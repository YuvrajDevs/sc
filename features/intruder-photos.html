Intruder photos: see who tried to open your vault

Wondering whether someone tried to get into your vault? Intruder photos answer that, with a picture of who it was.

In short

With intruder photos on, Sealcloak forgives the first two wrong patterns, then takes a front-camera photo of every wrong attempt from the third in a row. The photos are encrypted so that only the vault that turned the feature on can open them, and you see them after you unlock.

Turning it on

  1. In Settings, under Security, find Intruder photos and tap Turn on.
  2. Allow camera access when asked.
  3. That's it. Settings shows how many photos are waiting.

Why the first two are forgiven

Everyone slips when drawing a pattern sometimes. Photographing every single mistake would fill the folder with pictures of you. The growing waits after wrong tries are explained under pattern lock. From the third wrong pattern in a row, it's much more likely to be someone else.

Keep them locked, offline

Sealcloak encrypts your photos, videos and files on your phone. No account, no cloud, no internet permission.

Get it free on Google Play

Private even while locked

While the vault is locked, the app has no key to read your files. Intruder photos are sealed to a public key: the app can add a photo, but only your unlocked vault can open them. They never appear in your gallery or in a notification. After you unlock, a banner tells you how many wrong attempts were photographed.

The camera indicator

On Android 12 and newer, the phone shows its own green camera dot whenever any app uses the camera. That's an Android privacy feature, and no app can hide it. The photo is taken quickly, so the dot only appears briefly.

Use it lawfully

Rules about photographing other people differ by country. You're responsible for using this feature lawfully where you live. More in our terms.

Frequently asked questions

Does it take a photo on my own mistakes?

Only from the third wrong pattern in a row. Unlock successfully and the count starts again.

Where are intruder photos stored?

Encrypted in the app's private storage. They never go to your gallery and never leave the phone.

Can the intruder see that a photo was taken?

There's no sound or message from the app, but Android shows its camera indicator dot briefly while the camera is in use.

How many photos are kept?

The newest 20. Older ones are removed so a long attack can't fill your phone.
