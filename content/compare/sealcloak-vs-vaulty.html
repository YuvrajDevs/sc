Sealcloak vs Vaulty: a Vaulty alternative with no internet at all

Vaulty calls itself the original photo vault for Android, and it's still popular, with more than 10 million installs. It locks photos behind a numeric PIN, can pose as a calculator and offers free online backup. Sealcloak shares the disguise idea but keeps everything on the phone. Here's why that makes it the safer vault.

Quick answer

Sealcloak is the more secure Vaulty alternative. Instead of a numeric PIN and online backup, it uses a 5×5 pattern that becomes the encryption key, encrypts every file with AES-256-GCM, and has no internet permission, no account and no data sharing. It can be a working calculator too.

Why Sealcloak is more secure

Vaulty protects your vault with a numeric PIN, and its free online backup keeps a copy of your files on a server. Both are weak points Sealcloak was designed to avoid. A short PIN is easy to watch over your shoulder and has relatively few combinations. A server copy can be exposed in ways you'll never see.

Sealcloak's vault opens with a pattern on a 25-dot grid, and that pattern is the raw material for the encryption key: Argon2id stretches it into a key, so nothing usable is stored on the phone. Files are encrypted with AES-256-GCM. And because the app has no internet permission, there's no online copy, no account and no data shared with advertisers, by design.

What Sealcloak does differently

A numeric PIN is quick, but four or six digits can be watched over your shoulder and have far fewer combinations than a pattern on a 5×5 grid. Sealcloak's pattern lock needs at least 6 dots on a 25-dot grid, and wrong guesses trigger growing waits after the first four, which changing the phone's clock doesn't skip. The pattern isn't stored anywhere; it's turned into the encryption key with Argon2id.

Sealcloak also adds tools for the moments a vault is most tested. If someone forces you to open it, Coerce opens a believable decoy vault instead. If someone keeps guessing, intruder photos show you who. And because each of up to three vaults has its own pattern and nothing shows how many exist, you can keep one vault for everyday things and another nobody knows about.

The privacy difference

Vaulty's Play data safety section says it shares device identifiers for analytics and advertising. That doesn't mean anyone sees your photos, but it does mean the app talks to other companies. Its online backup also means a copy of your vault sits on a server.

Sealcloak can't do either. Android only lets apps reach the internet if they declare the internet permission, and Sealcloak doesn't. It has no ads, no analytics and no crash reporting, and it opts out of Android's automatic cloud backup of app data. Your photos exist in one place: encrypted on your phone. To protect against losing the phone, it makes encrypted backup files that you store on a memory card, computer or drive of your choosing; only your recovery phrase or a passphrase opens them.

Day to day

Both apps work like a private gallery once you're inside: albums or folders, a full-screen viewer and video playback. Sealcloak adds a few things beyond photos. Its private camera and document scanner save new pictures and PDF scans straight into the vault rather than the gallery, and it holds audio, documents and text notes too. Screenshots and screen recording are blocked inside the app, so nothing on screen can be captured by another app. When you want to send something, secure sharing packs files into a passphrase-locked package instead of sending them in the clear.

Moving from Vaulty to Sealcloak

  1. Set up Sealcloak: draw a pattern and write down the 9-word recovery phrase it shows you.
  2. In Vaulty, select your hidden photos and videos and unhide them, so they return to the gallery.
  3. In Sealcloak, add them to a vault. Sealcloak offers to keep the originals; open a few files to make sure they're all there.
  4. Delete the originals from the gallery and empty the gallery's trash, because many phones keep deleted photos for 30 days.
  5. Remove the files from Vaulty and its online backup, then uninstall it if you're done with it.

Why people switch to Sealcloak

  • No online copy: your photos exist only on your phone.
  • Stronger unlocking: a pattern-derived key and growing lockouts.
  • A decoy for pressure: Coerce opens a harmless vault when someone insists.
  • No ad tracking: nothing is shared with anyone, ever.

Verdict

Switching takes about ten minutes. Sealcloak gives you everything you'd want from a photo vault, including a calculator disguise, with stronger protection underneath and nothing online.
