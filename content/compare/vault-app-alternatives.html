Vault app alternatives for Android: 5 options compared

People look for Vault app alternatives for a handful of reasons: the ads, the account, the cloud upload, a forgotten password, or a new phone. Each reason points to a different replacement. Here are five, what each one is good at, where each one falls short, and how to move your photos across without losing any.

Quick answer

For free and simple, use what your phone already has: Google Photos Locked Folder, Safe folder in Files by Google, Samsung Secure Folder or Private space. They hide files well but mostly open with your screen lock or a short PIN. If you want a Vault replacement with its own lock, real per-file encryption, no account and no internet at all, Sealcloak is the offline option.

Why people leave Vault

Vault - Hide Pics, App Lock, still widely known as NQ Vault, does a lot in one app: hidden photos and videos, app lock, a private browser and its Cloud Space backup. That breadth is also the usual reason people start searching for something else. Its own Google Play data safety section, checked in November 2026, says the app:

  • collects photos, videos, files and audio for app functionality;
  • collects your email address, for account management among other uses;
  • shares approximate location with other companies for advertising or marketing;
  • shares device IDs and app activity for analytics and advertising.

None of that is hidden; the developer declares it. But if you installed a vault so that nobody else could see your photos, an app that lists those photos as collected data is a strange place to keep them. Other common complaints are simpler: too many prompts to upgrade, trouble getting back in after forgetting the password, and worry about what happens to Cloud Space copies after you stop using the app.

So before choosing a replacement, decide which of these matters to you. Someone who just wants fewer ads needs something different from someone whose partner knows their phone PIN.

Free alternatives already on your phone

1. Google Photos Locked Folder. If your photos already live in Google Photos, this is the shortest move. Items in Locked Folder disappear from the photo grid, albums, search and memories. Backup of Locked Folder is optional and off by default; Google's help page explains you can turn it on under Photos settings, then Backup, then Back up Locked Folder. The catch is the lock: it opens with your device screen lock. If someone knows your PIN or has watched you unlock, the folder is not a barrier for them. Our Locked Folder comparison goes deeper.

2. Safe folder in Files by Google. This takes any file type, not only photos, and you set a separate PIN or pattern for it. Google warns that if you forget that PIN or pattern you lose access, and resetting Safe folder permanently deletes what is inside. There is no recovery code, so write the lock down somewhere safe if you use it.

3. Samsung Secure Folder. On a Galaxy phone, Secure Folder is a separate space protected by Samsung Knox. It holds copies of whole apps as well as files, and it can be hidden from the apps screen. It is the strongest of the built-in choices, but it only exists on Samsung phones, and apps you put inside it can still go online and sync like any app. See Samsung hidden photos for where things end up on a Galaxy.

4. Android Private space. On Android 15 and newer, many phones offer Private space under Settings, in the Security and privacy section. It is built for apps: a second messaging app, a separate browser, anything you want paused and out of sight. It is less handy for individual photos, because pictures taken with your normal camera still land in your normal gallery.

These four are honest, free answers, and for many people one of them is enough. Their shared limit is that they are designed for hiding things from a quick glance, not for someone who knows your phone and is determined to look.

How to switch away from Vault without losing photos

The risky moment in any switch is the gap between taking files out of one app and putting them safely into the next. Go slowly and check as you go.

  1. Download Cloud Space items first. If you used Vault's cloud backup, make sure every photo and video is downloaded to the phone. Anything left only in the cloud is easy to forget.
  2. Restore to the gallery. In Vault, select your hidden photos and videos and restore or unhide them. Do this album by album if you have a lot, so you can see what has moved.
  3. Add them to the new home. Move them into Locked Folder, Safe folder or Secure Folder, or add them to a Sealcloak vault. Sealcloak offers to keep the originals; you delete them yourself afterwards.
  4. Open a sample. Check a few photos and at least one long video in the new app before deleting anything.
  5. Remove the leftovers. Delete the restored copies from the gallery, then empty the gallery's trash or bin, which can keep deleted items for weeks.
  6. Clean up Vault. Delete your data from Cloud Space, use the developer's deletion request if you want your account data removed, then uninstall Vault.

If you choose Sealcloak, write the recovery phrase on paper during setup and set up a scheduled encrypted backup the same day. A vault with no backup is one dropped phone away from empty.

What to check in any vault app before trusting it

There are dozens of apps like Vault on Google Play, and new ones appear every month. Whichever you look at, five minutes on its Play page tells you most of what you need:

  • Data safety section. Are photos and videos listed as collected? Is anything shared for advertising?
  • Account. Does it ask for an email or a sign-in? An account is something that can be phished or reset by someone else.
  • Encryption. Does it name a method such as AES-256, or only promise that files are hidden?
  • Recovery. What happens if you forget the lock? A password reset by email is convenient, but it means the company or your inbox can open the door.
  • Backups. Where do they go, and can you restore them on a new phone without the company's server?
