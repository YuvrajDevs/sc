Sealcloak vs LockMyPix: which LockMyPix alternative is more private?

LockMyPix is one of the few popular vaults that clearly encrypts your files instead of just hiding them, and it has more than 10 million installs to show for it. Sealcloak encrypts too. The difference is everything around the encryption: how the key is made, what the app shares, and whether it can reach the internet at all.

Quick answer

Sealcloak is the more private LockMyPix alternative. Both encrypt, but Sealcloak uses AES-256-GCM with a key made from your pattern, has no internet permission, never asks for an email, shows no ads and shares no data. It adds up to three hidden vaults, the Coerce decoy and intruder photos.

Why Sealcloak goes further

Encryption is the starting point for a real vault, not the finish line. What matters next is everything around it: how the key is protected, what the app can send, and how it behaves under pressure. LockMyPix's listing describes AES in CTR mode; its Play data safety section lists device IDs shared for advertising and an optional email.

Sealcloak tightens every one of those points. AES-256-GCM seals each file and detects tampering. Your pattern becomes the key through Argon2id and is never stored. Each vault has its own random master key protected with help from the Android Keystore. There's no internet permission, so no advertising IDs, no analytics and no email. And with up to three independent vaults, the Coerce decoy and intruder photos, it's built for real-life situations, not just for strangers.

Sealcloak's approach

Sealcloak deliberately offers fewer ways in. The vault opens with a pattern on a 5×5 grid; fingerprint or face unlock can be added for speed, but the pattern always works and is what the encryption key is made from. Argon2id turns that pattern into the key, which makes each guess slow and expensive, and after four wrong attempts Sealcloak adds growing waits that resetting the clock won't skip.

Where the apps really part ways is the internet. Sealcloak ships without Android's internet permission, so it has no ads, no analytics and no way to send anything anywhere. It never asks for an email. The Coerce decoy vault can, if you choose, also erase your other vaults when it's opened, which is off by default and needs you to type PERMIT to turn on.

AES-CTR and AES-GCM in plain words

Both apps use AES, the same cipher banks and governments rely on. The mode is the part after the dash. CTR mode keeps files secret, but on its own it doesn't notice if an encrypted file has been altered or damaged; apps that use it need a separate check for that. GCM mode keeps files secret and also includes a built-in seal, so if even one byte of an encrypted file changes, decryption fails instead of quietly producing a broken or tampered file.

Sealcloak uses AES-256-GCM for every file, and each vault has its own random master key protected with help from the Android Keystore. Neither choice is something you'll notice day to day; it matters for how robust the vault is when something goes wrong. More in how Sealcloak's encryption works.

Backups and new phones

Moving to a new phone is where vault apps are tested. With Sealcloak, you make an encrypted backup by hand or on a weekly or monthly schedule, copy the file to the new phone, and restore it with your recovery phrase or the backup's passphrase. Nothing depends on an account or a server still existing. With any vault, test a restore once before you need it.

What each app knows about you

Google Play's data safety section is where developers declare what an app collects and shares. LockMyPix lists device or other IDs shared for advertising or marketing, and optional email, crash logs and diagnostics collected. None of that includes your photos, which stay encrypted, but it does mean the app communicates with outside services.

Sealcloak declares no data collected and no data shared. Because it has no internet permission, that isn't just a policy; Android itself stops it from connecting. Read Sealcloak's security page for exactly how keys and files are handled.

From LockMyPix to Sealcloak, step by step

  1. Install Sealcloak, set your pattern, and copy the 9-word recovery phrase onto paper.
  2. In LockMyPix, open your albums, select everything and export or unhide it to the gallery.
  3. Add the files to a Sealcloak vault, keeping the same folder structure if you like.
  4. Open a sample of photos and videos in Sealcloak to confirm they all arrived.
  5. Delete the exported copies from the gallery, then empty the trash.
  6. Clear your LockMyPix vault and uninstall it once you're happy.

Also check where your old files sat on the SD card; delete any leftover exports there too. Our guide to locking folders on Android explains why leftovers matter.
