Why the calculator trick works

Nobody looks twice at a calculator. It sits on the home screen, it adds numbers when someone opens it, and it gives no hint that anything else is there. That's why calculator vaults are so popular with people who share a phone or hand it to family. Children borrowing a phone for games, a partner checking a message or a friend scrolling through holiday pictures all walk straight past it. The disguise is about avoiding questions, not about cryptography.

Calculator: Photo, Video Vault builds on that with useful extras: a fake password that opens harmless content, an incognito browser that downloads straight into the vault, direct sharing from the vault to social apps, and a face-down action that closes the app or opens another one if someone walks up.

What matters behind the calculator

A disguise only protects you until someone looks past it, for instance by checking the app list, plugging the phone into a computer or browsing storage with a file manager. At that point, the only thing between them and your photos is whether the files are encrypted, and how.

Sealcloak's calculator disguise is a layer on top of a fully encrypted vault. The calculator works normally. Your secret code of 4 to 8 digits, held on the equals key for two seconds, only opens the door; you still need your pattern or fingerprint, and the files themselves are AES-256-GCM encrypted with a key derived from that pattern. Someone who finds the files without it gets unreadable data.

Ads and data in free calculator vaults

Most calculator vault apps are free and supported by ads. In Google Play's data safety section, Calculator: Photo, Video Vault lists device IDs shared for advertising or marketing, and app interactions, crash logs and diagnostics collected for analytics. It also states data can't be deleted on request. None of that means anyone sees your photos, but it's an unusual trade for an app whose whole purpose is privacy.

Sealcloak has no ads and no analytics. It doesn't have Android's internet permission, so the system won't let it connect to anything. It never asks for an email, and there's no account to delete because there's no account at all.

Choosing a calculator vault safely

  1. Check for encryption in the listing. "Hidden" is not the same as encrypted.
  2. Read the data safety section on Google Play: what's shared, and why.
  3. Look at permissions. A vault doesn't need your contacts or location.
  4. Test recovery. Find out what happens if you forget the code, before it happens.
  5. Test a backup on another phone if you can, or at least make one.

Sealcloak answers the recovery question with a 9-word recovery phrase for each vault, and backups are encrypted files you keep.

Beyond the calculator: other disguises

A calculator is the most common disguise, which is also its weakness: people who know about vault apps know to look for an extra calculator. If your phone already has a built-in calculator, a second one on the home screen can stand out. Sealcloak's alternative is a custom icon: any picture and any name, so the app can blend in as whatever fits your home screen. Either way, the vault behind it is the same encrypted vault.

Remember the other places a vault can give itself away: notifications, the recent apps screen and storage usage in Settings. Sealcloak blocks screenshots inside the app, so the recent apps preview stays blank instead of showing your photos.
