Sealcloak vs AppLock: the AppLock alternative for private photos

AppLock by DoMobile is one of the most installed privacy apps on Android, with more than 100 million downloads. It puts a lock in front of WhatsApp, the Gallery, Settings and any app you pick, and it can hide pictures and videos. Sealcloak doesn't lock apps. It does something an app lock can't: it encrypts your private files so they're unreadable to anyone but you.

Quick answer

For private photos and documents, Sealcloak is the safer choice. AppLock places a lock screen in front of apps and hides media, while its data safety section lists email, installed apps and device IDs shared, including for advertising. Sealcloak encrypts every file with AES-256-GCM from your own pattern, has no internet permission and shares nothing.

A lock screen isn't encryption

An app lock works by showing its own lock screen whenever a protected app opens. That's handy for stopping a friend from opening your chats. But the data inside those apps isn't changed: it's still stored the same way on the phone, and the lock only works while the app lock itself is running and can't be bypassed. AppLock's own FAQ even lists several ways to open the app when its icon is hidden, which shows how many doors a lock-screen approach has to guard.

Sealcloak protects the files rather than the door. Every photo, video, document, audio file and note is encrypted with AES-256-GCM before it's stored. The key comes from your pattern through Argon2id and is never saved, so there's nothing on the phone that opens your files without you. If someone copies the storage, uses a file manager, or connects the phone to a computer, they find encrypted data.

What an app lock shares

AppLock is free and supported by ads, and its Play data safety section reflects that. It lists email address, app interactions, the list of installed apps, crash logs, diagnostics and device identifiers as shared with other companies, for purposes including advertising, analytics and account management. The list of apps on your phone can say a lot about you on its own.

Sealcloak has no internet permission, so it can't share anything with anyone. There are no ads, no analytics, no crash reports and no account. Your vault is between you and your phone.

Use both, for different jobs

If you rely on an app lock for WhatsApp or Instagram, keep using one: many phones now include app lock in Settings, and recent Android versions offer Private space for whole apps. Our guide to hiding apps on Android covers each brand. Then move the files you truly can't risk (private photos, ID scans, medical documents) into Sealcloak, where they're encrypted and the app can never go online.

Protection for when the lock isn't enough

People who need an app lock are often protecting themselves from someone close by: a partner, a parent, a flatmate. Those situations need more than a lock screen. Sealcloak gives you up to three separate vaults, each with its own pattern and none visible from the others. Coerce opens a harmless decoy vault if someone insists you unlock. Intruder photos capture whoever keeps guessing, from the third wrong pattern in a row. And the calculator disguise makes the app look like nothing more than a calculator.

Forgotten passwords and recovery

App locks usually let you recover a forgotten password through the app itself or an email, which means there's a reset path that isn't only yours. Sealcloak has no reset path that anyone else controls. Each vault gets a 9-word recovery phrase when you create it; write it on paper and keep it away from the phone. It's the only spare key. To protect against a lost or broken phone, Sealcloak makes encrypted backups to a folder you choose, by hand or on a schedule.

Moving your hidden media

  1. Install Sealcloak, set a pattern of six or more dots and write down the recovery phrase.
  2. In AppLock's photo and video vault, unhide your items so they return to the gallery.
  3. Add them to a Sealcloak vault and check that a few open properly.
  4. Delete the gallery copies and empty the gallery's trash, which keeps deleted photos for weeks on many phones.
  5. Keep AppLock only if you still need it to lock apps.

Bottom line

AppLock is a lock screen for your apps. Sealcloak is encryption for your files. If what you're protecting is private photos and documents, you want them unreadable, offline and out of advertising networks, and that's exactly what Sealcloak is built for.
