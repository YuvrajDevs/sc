Gallery Vault, by ThinkYeah, is one of the most installed vault apps on Android, with more than 50 million downloads. It does a lot: encrypted hiding, a private browser, a video downloader, SD card support and break-in alerts. Sealcloak does one thing, keeping your files safe, with nothing online. Here's why that matters.

Quick answer

Sealcloak is the more secure Gallery Vault alternative. It's a focused vault with no internet permission, no account, no location or ad data, and every file encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a key made from your pattern. Hidden vaults, a decoy, intruder photos and a calculator disguise are built in.

Why focus makes Sealcloak safer

Gallery Vault bundles a vault with a web browser, a video downloader and advertising. Every one of those needs the internet, and its Play data safety section lists approximate location and device IDs collected or shared, including for advertising. That's a lot of moving parts around your most private files.

Sealcloak does one job and does it with nothing attached. No browser, no downloader, no ads, no analytics and no internet permission, so Android itself won't let the app connect. What remains is the vault: AES-256-GCM on every file, a pattern-derived key, separate vaults with separate keys, and protection for the moments that matter most.

Why Sealcloak stays small

Every feature that talks to the internet is also a way for data to leave the phone. A browser and a downloader need network access; ads and analytics need it too. Sealcloak removes all of that. Without the internet permission, Android won't let it connect anywhere, so the only place your files can exist is encrypted on your phone and in the backups you make yourself.

What's left is a vault done carefully. A 5×5 pattern becomes your encryption key through Argon2id. Up to three independent vaults each have their own pattern and key, and nothing in the app reveals how many exist. Coerce opens a decoy when you're pressured, and erase after wrong patterns is available for people who need it, off by default and protected by typing PERMIT.

Location and advertising data

Gallery Vault's data safety section lists approximate location collected for advertising or marketing, plus device IDs, diagnostics and app interactions shared with other companies for analytics, advertising and account management. That's common for free apps supported by ads, and it doesn't mean your hidden photos are shared. But if your reason for using a vault is privacy, it's worth knowing what the app itself reports.

Sealcloak declares no data collected or shared, and has no advertising. It even blocks screenshots and screen recording inside the app so other apps can't capture what's on screen.

Built for the moment someone asks to see

A vault is tested when someone is standing next to you. Sealcloak's Coerce opens a believable decoy vault with its own pattern. Up to three vaults can exist without any sign of how many. Intruder photos record who keeps guessing. And the app can be a working calculator. Each of these works offline and leaves no trace outside the app. Screenshots and screen recording are blocked inside Sealcloak too, so the recent apps screen shows a blank card instead of your photos.

Hidden vs encrypted: how to check any vault

Whichever app you use, it's worth knowing the difference. A hidden file has only been moved or renamed so the gallery skips it; plug the phone into a computer, or open a file manager, and it can often be viewed. An encrypted file is scrambled with a key, so even someone who copies it sees nothing useful. Both apps here encrypt, which is the important part.

A quick test for any vault: hide a photo, then open your phone's Files app and look through the storage folders. If you can find and open the photo there, it's only hidden. With Sealcloak, files live in the app's private storage as encrypted data, and backups are encrypted files that only your recovery phrase or a passphrase can open. How to switch

  1. Install Sealcloak, draw a pattern and write down your recovery phrase.
  2. In Gallery Vault, select your files and unhide them; they're decrypted back to normal storage.
  3. Add them to Sealcloak. Use folders to recreate your albums.
  4. Open some files to check they're complete, especially long videos.
  5. Delete the unhidden copies from your gallery and SD card, and empty the trash.
  6. Set up a scheduled encrypted backup so you're covered if the phone is lost.

The verdict

Downloads and browsing can live in any app you like; your most private photos, ID scans and documents deserve a place with no network access at all. For a vault that collects nothing, shows no ads and cannot reach the internet, with strong encryption and real-world protection built in, Sealcloak is the safer home for your private files.
