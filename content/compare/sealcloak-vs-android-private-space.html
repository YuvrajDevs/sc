Sealcloak vs Android Private space: apps apart, files protected

Android 15 introduced Private space: a separate, lockable area where apps can live out of sight. It's a welcome addition to Android, and it's great for a second messaging app or a private browser. But it's built around apps, not individual files, it needs a recent Android version, and it doesn't help when someone is pressuring you to unlock. That's where Sealcloak comes in.

Quick answer

Use Private space to separate whole apps. Use Sealcloak to protect your private photos, videos and documents: every file encrypted with AES-256-GCM from its own pattern, up to three hidden vaults, a Coerce decoy, intruder photos and encrypted backups you can restore anywhere. It works on Android 8.0 and newer and has no internet permission.

Apps versus files

Private space shines when you want whole apps kept apart: a second WhatsApp, a separate browser, a dating app nobody else should see. When it's locked, those apps are hidden and paused, and their notifications don't show. What it doesn't do is give you a gallery-like vault for individual files. Photos you take with your normal camera still land in your normal gallery, and documents still sit in normal folders unless you deliberately move them into an app inside the space.

Sealcloak is that vault. Add photos, videos, PDFs, audio and notes, or capture new ones with its private camera and document scanner, and every file is encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a key made from your pattern. You can find, view and organise them like a gallery, without them ever appearing in the main one.

When someone wants you to unlock

A private space is one space. If someone knows it exists and demands you open it, there's nothing else to show them. Sealcloak was designed with that moment in mind. Up to three vaults can live in the app, each opened by its own pattern, and none reveals the others. Coerce opens a harmless, believable decoy vault. Intruder photos capture whoever keeps guessing. And the app can be a working calculator, so there's nothing that looks like a vault.

Works on the phone you already have

Private space needs Android 15 or newer, and phone makers roll it out on their own schedules. Many popular phones in India, Indonesia, Brazil and elsewhere run older versions for years. Sealcloak runs on Android 8.0 and newer, on every brand, so the same protection works across your family's phones regardless of model.

A backup that moves with you

Changing phones is when private files most often get lost. Sealcloak makes encrypted backups by hand or on a weekly or monthly schedule, to a folder you choose. Copy the file to your new phone, install Sealcloak, and restore with your recovery phrase or the passphrase you set. No account and no cloud are involved: Sealcloak has no internet permission.

Screenshots, recents and other leaks

Private files leak in small ways: a thumbnail in the recent apps screen, a screenshot someone takes while you scroll, a photo that lands in the gallery before you move it. Sealcloak blocks screenshots and screen recording inside the app, so recents shows a blank card. Its camera saves straight into the vault. And because it has no internet permission, there's no sync, no cloud preview and no analytics that could reveal anything.

Family phones and older devices

Plenty of households hand phones down or share a tablet. A feature that only exists on the newest Android version doesn't help the person using last year's phone. Because Sealcloak works on Android 8.0 and newer, everyone can use the same vault app.

Using both together

  1. Set up Private space for the apps you want separate, if your phone offers it.
  2. Install Sealcloak for your private photos, videos and documents; inside Private space if you want two layers.
  3. Use a vault pattern that's different from your screen lock and Private space lock.
  4. Write down each vault's recovery phrase and keep it away from the phone.
  5. Schedule an encrypted backup and copy it off the phone now and then.
