Sealcloak vs Hide Something: the encrypted Hide Something alternative

Hide Something is a friendly, popular way to tuck photos and videos away, with more than 10 million installs, Google Drive backup and even a way to browse your hidden gallery from a computer. Sealcloak is built around a stricter idea: your private media should be encrypted, should stay on your phone, and should never be reachable over a network.

Quick answer

Sealcloak is the more secure Hide Something alternative. Every file is encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a key made from your pattern, the app has no internet permission, there's no Google Drive copy and nothing is shared. Hide Something's own data safety section lists approximate location and device IDs shared for advertising.

Hidden isn't the same as protected

Hide Something's listing talks about hiding: media disappears from the gallery and reappears behind your fingerprint, pattern or PIN. That stops someone scrolling your gallery. It doesn't say how the files themselves are protected if someone looks elsewhere, for instance with a file manager, a computer connection or a backup copy.

Sealcloak treats every file as if someone will eventually find it. Photos, videos, documents, audio, notes and scans are encrypted with AES-256-GCM as they're added. Without your pattern, which is turned into the key by Argon2id and never stored, the files are unreadable data. And GCM seals each one, so a tampered file is rejected rather than opened.

Why we left out Drive and the computer viewer

Viewing your secret gallery on a computer and backing it up to Google Drive are convenient. They also mean your private media travels across a network and sits in an online account. Hide Something's data safety section also lists approximate location, device IDs, app interactions and diagnostics shared with other companies, mostly for advertising and analytics.

Sealcloak has none of these, because it has no internet permission. Android itself prevents it from connecting. There are no ads, no analytics, no crash reports and no account. That's a deliberate trade: in exchange for giving up remote access, you get a vault that can't leak over a network, ever.

Backups you control

You still need protection against losing your phone. Sealcloak creates encrypted backup files by hand, or every week or month, in a folder you choose. Copy them to your computer or a memory card. They're encrypted, so a backup on a shared computer reveals nothing, and only your recovery phrase or the passphrase you set can restore it on a new phone.

Built for the people around you

Hide Something lets you make collections for different friends and family. Sealcloak goes further with up to three separate vaults, each opened by its own pattern, and nothing in the app shows how many exist. Open your everyday vault when someone's watching; the others stay invisible. If someone insists, Coerce opens a believable decoy. Intruder photos show you who tried to guess, and the calculator disguise keeps the app from looking like a vault at all.

Switching in a few minutes

  1. Install Sealcloak, draw a pattern of six or more dots, and write down your recovery phrase.
  2. In Hide Something, select your hidden media and unhide it so it returns to the gallery.
  3. Add it to Sealcloak. Make folders that match your old collections if you like.
  4. Open a few photos and videos to check they're all there.
  5. Delete the gallery copies and empty the gallery's recently deleted folder.
  6. Delete Hide Something's Google Drive backup, so no copy stays online, then uninstall it.

Everyday use

Inside, Sealcloak works like a private gallery: folders, search, a full-screen viewer, slideshow and video playback, with multi-select to move, copy or export. It holds more than photos and videos, too: PDFs and other documents, audio and text notes. The private camera and document scanner save new pictures and scans straight into the vault, so they never sit in the gallery first. Screenshots and screen recording are blocked inside the app, which also keeps your photos out of the recent apps screen.

When you need to send something, secure sharing wraps files in a passphrase-locked package, so the person receiving it needs the passphrase to open it. Nothing goes through our servers, because there aren't any.

Bottom line

Hide Something makes hiding easy and puts copies in the cloud. Sealcloak makes your media unreadable and keeps it off every network, with a stronger lock, hidden vaults, a decoy and backups only you can open.
