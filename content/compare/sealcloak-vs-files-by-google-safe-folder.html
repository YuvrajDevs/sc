Sealcloak vs Files by Google Safe folder: a safe folder alternative

Safe folder, inside the Files by Google app, is a quick way to lock away a few files with a PIN or pattern. For a handful of documents on a phone nobody touches, it's convenient. When the files really matter, or someone around you is curious, you need encryption you can see, more than one vault, a way to survive a lost phone, and protection when someone pushes you to open it.

Quick answer

Choose Sealcloak for anything you can't afford to lose or expose. It encrypts every file with AES-256-GCM using a key made from your pattern, offers up to three hidden vaults, a decoy, intruder photos and a calculator disguise, and makes encrypted backups you can restore on a new phone. It has no internet permission and no account.

Why a locked folder isn't a vault

A locked folder answers one question: can someone browse to these files without the code? A vault has to answer several more. Are the files themselves unreadable if they're copied? What if you forget the code? What happens when the phone breaks? What if someone stands over you and insists you open it? Safe folder is designed to be simple, so it leaves most of those to you.

Sealcloak is built around all of them. Files are encrypted with AES-256-GCM before they're stored, so a copy of the storage reveals nothing. The key comes from your pattern through Argon2id, a method designed to make each guess slow, and after four wrong patterns the app adds waits that grow each time and can't be skipped by changing the clock.

Not losing everything with the phone

Locked folders tend to live and die with one app on one phone. Before you uninstall the app, reset it or switch phones, you have to remember to move everything out. Forget, and the files can be lost. Sealcloak gives you a proper path instead: encrypted backups by hand or on a weekly or monthly schedule, saved to a folder you choose, and a recovery phrase for each vault. On a new phone, install Sealcloak, restore the backup, and everything is back.

For the people around you

One folder with one code is easy to demand. Sealcloak lets you keep up to three separate vaults with separate patterns, and nothing reveals how many exist. If someone insists, Coerce opens a harmless decoy. Intruder photos capture whoever keeps guessing. And with the calculator disguise or a custom icon, there's no obvious "safe" to point at.

Photos, documents and everything in between

Safe folder is part of a file manager, so it's good at documents and downloads. Sealcloak handles those and is also a private gallery: photos and videos with a full-screen viewer and slideshow, audio, text notes, and a private camera and document scanner that save directly into the vault, never touching the gallery. Folders, search and multi-select keep it organised, and screenshots are blocked inside the app.

No internet, no account

Sealcloak doesn't have Android's internet permission, so it can't send files or data anywhere, and it never asks you to sign in. Nothing about your vault exists outside your phone and the backups you choose to make.

A quick check of any locked folder

Here's a simple test for any built-in or third-party locker. Ask three questions. If someone copied the phone's storage, could they read the files? If the app were uninstalled or reset, where would the files go? If someone stood over you and demanded the code, would you have anything to show them except the real thing? Sealcloak's answers are no, into your encrypted backup, and yes, the Coerce decoy vault.

Moving from Safe folder

  1. Install Sealcloak, draw a pattern and write down the 9-word recovery phrase.
  2. Open Files by Google, go to Safe folder, select your files and move them out.
  3. Add them to a Sealcloak vault, recreating folders if you like.
  4. Open a few to check them, then delete the copies outside the vault and empty any trash.
  5. Make your first encrypted backup and copy it off the phone.
