Password protect a PDF

Lock a PDF with a password before you email or share it. The result is a normal PDF: Adobe, Chrome, phones and every PDF reader ask for the password before showing anything.

Quick answer

To password protect a PDF, choose it above, type and confirm a password, choose whether printing, copying and editing are allowed, and tap Protect PDF. The file is encrypted with AES-256 on your device and opens in any PDF reader once the password is entered.

How to protect a PDF

  1. Choose the PDF.
  2. Type a strong password twice. A random passphrase is easy to read out over the phone.
  3. Choose whether the recipient may print, copy text or edit.
  4. Tap Protect PDF and download the protected copy.
  5. Send the PDF, and share the password a different way, never in the same email.

How strong is it?

The PDF is encrypted with AES-256, the strongest standard PDF encryption, using qpdf, a widely used open-source PDF tool that runs here in your browser. Without the password, the pages can't be read. As always, the password is the weak point: use 12 or more characters, and nothing guessable like a date of birth.

Protect PDF vs encrypt a file

A protected PDF opens in any PDF reader, which is what most people need. Our encrypt a file tool works for any file type, and the recipient decrypts it on our page. Print and copy limits are honoured by most readers, but they're restrictions, not encryption; the password is what protects the content.
