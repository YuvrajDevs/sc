Reduce photo size for email

Phone photos are often 3 to 8 MB each, so a handful can blow past an email's attachment limit. Shrink them here first and they'll send in one go, still looking sharp.

Quick answer

To reduce a photo's size for email, choose it above with the target at 1000 KB (1 MB) and download the result. Five photos at 1 MB each fit comfortably under Gmail's 25 MB limit. The photos are compressed on your device and never uploaded.

Attachment limits

Email serviceAttachment limit
Gmail25 MB per email
Outlook.com20 MB per email
Yahoo Mail25 MB per email

The limit counts the whole email, and attachments grow by about a third when they're encoded for sending, so aim well below it. As a rule of thumb, photos of about 1 MB each look great on any screen and let you send a dozen or more at once.

Picking the right size

  • For viewing on a phone or computer: 500 KB to 1 MB per photo is plenty.
  • For printing at 6 × 4 inches: keep at least 1600 pixels on the long side and about 1 to 2 MB.
  • For a form or portal: use the exact limit it gives, for example 100 KB.

Why compress here rather than in an online converter

Most compression websites upload your photos to their servers to process them. For holiday snaps that may not matter, but for ID scans, family photos or anything personal, it's an unnecessary copy on someone else's computer. This tool works inside your browser: the photo is never sent anywhere. Before sharing, you can also remove the hidden location that phone photos carry.

Private by design

The compress image tool on this page runs entirely inside your browser tab. Your files are opened, processed and saved on your own device and are never sent to a server, so there's nothing for anyone to store, scan or leak. There's no sign-up, no watermark and no limit, and once the page has loaded it keeps working even if you switch off your internet.
