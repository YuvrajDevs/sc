How long should a password be?

Length matters, but randomness matters more. A random 16-character password or a five-word random passphrase is strong; a long password made of a name and a year is not.

Quick answer

Use at least 16 random characters for passwords stored in a password manager, and a passphrase of at least five random words for ones you must remember. Eight characters is the bare minimum many sites accept, and it isn't enough for important accounts.

Practical lengths

TypeGood lengthWhy
Random characters (manager)16 to 20Very strong; you never type it
Random passphrase (memorised)5 to 6 wordsStrong and easy to type
PIN for a phone6+ digitsProtected by lockouts, not length alone

Randomness beats tricks

Attackers try common words, names, dates and substitutions like @ for a first. "Summer2024!" is long-ish but guessable. A passphrase like five random words from a big list has around 65 bits of randomness, and every extra word multiplies the guesses needed by 7,776. Length isn't everything

  • Never reuse a password; one leak can unlock everything else.
  • Turn on two-step verification for email and banking.
  • Use a password manager so long random passwords cost you no effort.
