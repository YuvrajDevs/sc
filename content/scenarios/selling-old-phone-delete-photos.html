Selling your phone: how to delete photos before selling it

Selling, trading in or handing down an old phone is a good idea, as long as your photos don't go with it. Deleting them from the gallery isn't enough. The good news is that on a modern Android phone, a proper factory reset makes your old data unrecoverable. The order you do things in is what matters.

What to do now

Back up your photos and move any vault to your new phone, and check the backup opens. Then remove your Google account and any phone-maker account, take out the SIM and memory card, and factory reset the phone. On modern Android, a reset after encryption leaves nothing readable behind.

In the right order

  1. Back up everything. Ordinary photos: Google Photos, a computer or your new phone's transfer tool. Chats: WhatsApp's own backup. Private photos in a vault: the vault's own backup.
  2. Prove the backup works. Open a few photos on the new phone or computer. Restore your vault on the new phone and open it. Only then go on.
  3. Remove your accounts. In Settings, under Accounts or Passwords and accounts, remove your Google account and any Samsung, Xiaomi or other brand account. This lifts the factory reset lock so the buyer can set the phone up.
  4. Take out the SIM and memory card. Memory cards are often unencrypted and keep their own copies of photos; keep the card or wipe it on a computer.
  5. Factory reset. Settings, then System (or General management), then Reset options, then Erase all data. Confirm with your screen lock.
  6. Remove the old phone from your Google account's device list.

Why a reset is enough on modern phones

Phones that launched with Android 10 or newer encrypt their storage, and most phones from the years before did too. Your photos are stored scrambled, and the key that unscrambles them is kept in the phone's secure hardware. A factory reset throws that key away. The scrambled data may physically remain until it's overwritten, but without the key it's just noise. That's why recovery tools that work on old memory cards don't work on a reset modern phone.

For a very old phone, check Settings, under Security, for an Encrypt phone option. If it's there and not already on, encrypt the phone first, then reset it.

Trade-in and repair-and-resale programmes

Carrier and manufacturer trade-in schemes usually wipe phones they receive, but you can't see that happen, and some phones are resold as they are. Reset the phone yourself before it leaves your hands, every time. If the phone won't turn on, you can't reset it, so ask the trade-in service how they handle data and whether they certify erasure. Selling it yourself online

Private sales add one more privacy step that people miss: the listing photos. Pictures of the phone taken at home can carry the exact location in their hidden data, and a reflection in a dark screen can show your face or your room. Photograph the phone against a plain background, and run the pictures through our free photo location remover before you post them. When you meet the buyer, let them check the phone after the reset, at the setup screen, not while your accounts are still on it.

Giving a phone to family

Handing a phone to a child or a parent feels safer, but the same steps apply. Children explore everything, and family members can sync your old accounts by accident. Reset it and let them set it up as their own. Hand over the charger and box too, but keep your memory card. If it's for a child, set it up with parental controls from the start.

Bottom line

Back up, check the backup, remove accounts, take out the SIM and card, then reset. On a modern Android phone, that's all it takes for your old photos to be gone for good.
