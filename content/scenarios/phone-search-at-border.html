Phone search at the border: travelling with private photos

More and more travellers are asked to show their phones when crossing borders. The rules differ from country to country, and officials often have broad powers. You can't control the rules, but you can control what you carry. A little preparation means a phone check is an inconvenience, not a crisis.

What to do now

Before you travel, check the official rules for your destination, back up your phone, and remove what you don't need for the trip. Keep private photos and documents encrypted behind a separate lock, turn off cloud sync you don't need, and if you're asked about your phone, stay calm, polite and honest.

Understand the rules first

Many countries allow border or customs officers to inspect electronic devices, sometimes without the suspicion a police search would need. Some can ask you to unlock a device; some can keep it for a time. Refusing can have consequences, such as delays or refused entry for visitors. Never lie to officials or give false information. Look up official government guidance for the country you're entering, and get legal advice if you have specific concerns, such as work as a journalist or lawyer.

This page is general information, not legal advice.

Before you travel

  1. Back up everything at home: photos, chats and vault backups, to a computer or drive you leave behind.
  2. Travel with less. Remove photos, files and apps you won't need on the trip. Empty the gallery's trash.
  3. Check your cloud. Apps that sync can show cloud content even after you delete local copies. Sign out of what you don't need, or turn off sync for the trip.
  4. Encrypt what you keep. Private photos and documents should live in an encrypted vault, not the gallery.
  5. Update your phone and set a strong screen lock.
  6. Note your phone's IMEI (dial *#06#) and keep it with your travel papers.

What to keep, and where

ItemWhere it's best on a trip
Passport and visa copiesEncrypted vault, plus paper copies

If you're asked

  • Stay calm and polite. Ask what's required and why, if you're unsure.
  • Don't lie, and don't hand over passwords for accounts you weren't asked about.
  • Note officers' names or badge numbers, and ask for any receipt if a device is kept.
  • Afterwards, change passwords you shared and check your accounts for activity.

After the trip

Restore anything you removed from your home backup, re-enable sync, and review your accounts' security pages. If your phone was taken out of your sight, consider changing your screen lock. Our guide to protecting photos on a lost phone covers similar account steps.
