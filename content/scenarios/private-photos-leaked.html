Private photos leaked: what to do now

Finding out that your private photos have been shared, or that someone is threatening to share them, is frightening. It is not your fault, and you are not alone: this happens to many people, and there are real steps that work. Take a breath and go through them in order.

What to do now

Don't pay or send anything to someone threatening you. Save evidence: screenshots, links, usernames and dates. Report the images to each platform and ask for removal, and contact the police and a specialist support service. Then secure your phone and accounts. Talk to someone you trust; you don't have to handle this alone.

Right now

  1. Don't pay, and don't send more. Paying rarely ends threats; it often leads to more demands.
  2. Stop replying, but don't delete the conversation yet.
  3. Save evidence. Screenshot messages, profiles and posts, including usernames, links and dates. Store the evidence somewhere safe.
  4. Report and block the account on the platform where it happened.
  5. Tell someone you trust. A friend, family member or counsellor can help you think clearly.

Getting images removed

Most major platforms prohibit sharing intimate images without consent and will remove them when reported. Use the platform's reporting option for non-consensual or intimate images, not just general harassment. Search engines also have forms to remove such images from results. Specialist organisations in many countries can help report images across several sites, and some services can create a digital fingerprint of an image so participating platforms block it from being uploaded.

Getting help

WhoHow they help
PoliceIn many countries, sharing or threatening to share intimate images is a crime

A lawyerAdvice on civil or criminal options where you live

If you are under 18, tell a trusted adult and report it; there are dedicated services for young people, and the images are illegal for anyone to share.

Find out how it happened, and close the door

  • Accounts: change passwords for email, cloud and social accounts, turn on two-step verification, and sign out of all other sessions.
  • Phone: change your screen lock, check for unknown apps and linked devices; see signs someone went through your phone.
  • Cloud: check Google Photos and other backups for shared albums or links you didn't create.
  • Old phones: make sure any phone you sold or handed in was reset properly; see selling an old phone.
