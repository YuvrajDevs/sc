Personal photos on a work phone

Work and personal life mix on phones more than anywhere else. Maybe you use your own phone for work email, or your employer gave you a phone you also use for everything else. Either way, it's worth knowing what your employer can see, and how to keep your private photos out of it.

What to do now

Find out whether your phone is company-owned or your own with a work profile. On a company-owned phone, move personal photos off it, because it can be monitored, wiped or handed back. On your own phone, keep work apps inside the work profile and personal photos in your personal profile, ideally in an encrypted vault.

Two kinds of work phone

Your own phone with a work profileCompany-owned, fully managed
Who owns itYouYour employer
What the employer managesThe work profile (briefcase apps)Potentially the whole device
Your personal photosIn your personal profileOn a device you don't control
If you leave the jobWork profile removedPhone handed back or wiped

Look in Settings for "Work profile" or a briefcase icon on some apps, or for a message saying the device is managed by your organisation. Your IT team or device policy will tell you what's monitored.

On your own phone

  1. Keep work in the work profile. Install work apps through it, and don't use personal apps for work files.
  2. Keep personal photos personal. Use your personal camera and gallery, not the work profile's.
  3. Watch the share sheet. Sharing between profiles can move files where you didn't mean them to go.
  4. Pause work apps outside hours if your phone allows it; many show a "Work apps" toggle in quick settings.

On a company-owned phone

  1. Read the device policy. It should say what's logged and whether personal use is allowed.
  2. Move personal photos off to your own phone or computer, and empty the gallery's trash.
  3. Sign out of personal accounts such as your personal Google account and messaging apps.
  4. Before leaving the job, check again: the phone may be wiped or reissued.

Keeping private files private on your own phone

Even on your own phone, colleagues borrow it, screens get shared in meetings, and screenshots get sent to the wrong chat. Keeping your most private photos and documents in an encrypted vault means they never show up in your gallery, your photo widgets or a screen share. Sealcloak encrypts every file with a key made from its own pattern, blocks screenshots inside the app, and has no internet permission, so nothing syncs anywhere. Install it in your personal profile, not the work one.

The custom icon option lets the app blend in on your home screen, which helps when your phone is on the meeting-room table.

Screen sharing and presentations

  • Turn on Do Not Disturb before presenting from your phone.
  • Close the gallery and chats so they don't appear in recent apps.
  • Turn off photo widgets and "memories" on your home screen.

Bottom line

Know which kind of work phone you have. Keep personal photos off company-owned devices, keep work inside the work profile on your own, and keep what's truly private encrypted behind a separate lock. For more, see how to hide photos on Android and how Sealcloak works.
