How to know if someone went through my phone

Maybe your phone was in a different place from where you left it, or an app was open that you didn't open, or someone mentioned something they could only have seen on your screen. That uneasy feeling is worth checking. Android keeps more clues than most people realise, and there's a lot you can do to make sure it doesn't happen again.

What to do now

Open Digital Wellbeing (or Screen time) and look for app use at times you weren't using the phone. Check recent apps and your notification history. In your Google account, review recent activity and signed-in devices. Then change your screen lock, and move private photos into an encrypted vault with its own separate lock.

Five places to look

  1. Screen time. Open Settings, then Digital Wellbeing and parental controls (on some phones it's called Screen time). Tap the chart for today or yesterday and look at the hour-by-hour use. Gallery, WhatsApp or Instagram use while you were asleep or at work stands out.
  2. Recent apps. Swipe up and hold, or tap the square button. Apps appear in the order they were last used. Something on top that you didn't open is a clue.
  3. Notification history. On Android 11 and newer, Settings, then Notifications, then Notification history shows notifications from the past day, including ones someone may have opened or dismissed.
  4. Messaging apps. Read receipts on messages you haven't read, chats marked as read, or deleted messages in a chat's history. In WhatsApp, Linked devices shows if your account is open on a computer.
  5. Your Google account. In your account's Security page, check Recent security activity and Your devices. On myactivity.google.com you can see searches and app activity with times.

Signs it may be more than a quick look

A one-off look is upsetting. Ongoing monitoring is different, and the signs are different too:

  • Apps you don't recognise, especially ones with device administrator or accessibility access (check Settings, then Security, for device admin apps, and Accessibility for services you didn't turn on).
  • Google Play Protect warnings, or a battery that drains much faster than before.
  • Someone knowing where you've been, through shared location in Google Maps or a family app.
  • WhatsApp, Telegram or email open on a device you don't own.

Run a Play Protect scan from the Play Store (your profile icon, then Play Protect). Remove any unknown apps, turn off location sharing you didn't set up, and sign out other devices from WhatsApp and your Google account.

Why a second lock matters

The people most likely to go through your phone are the ones close to you, and they often know your screen lock already, or saw it over your shoulder. Once the phone is unlocked, the gallery, its hidden album and most "locked" folders are a tap away.

What a hidden album doesn't protect

Many people already use the gallery's hidden album, Google Photos' Locked Folder or a "private" folder. Those help against a casual scroll, but each has limits worth knowing. A hidden album is usually one tap away in the gallery menu for anyone holding the unlocked phone. Locked Folder often opens with the same screen lock or fingerprint that unlocks the phone. And files hidden by renaming a folder still show up in a file manager. If the person you're worried about knows your screen lock, use something with a different lock.

Talking about it

If the person is a partner, family member or friend, how you handle it depends on the relationship. Sometimes a calm conversation about boundaries is enough. Sometimes it isn't safe to confront someone. Trust your sense of that. Collect what you found (screenshots of screen time, for example) before you change anything, in case you need it later.

When it's controlling or abusive

Regularly checking a partner's phone, demanding passwords, tracking location or reading messages can be a form of controlling behaviour. If you feel watched, afraid or not free to say no, you're not overreacting. Domestic abuse helplines can give confidential advice, including how to secure your phone without alerting the other person. Changing things suddenly can sometimes raise risk, so a helpline can help you plan. In an emergency, call your local emergency number.

Bottom line

Screen time, recent apps, notification history and your Google account usually tell you whether someone has been through your phone. Change your screen lock, remove access you didn't approve, and keep your most private photos behind a second, encrypted lock.
