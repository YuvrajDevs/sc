Hide apps on Home and Apps screens on Samsung: how it works, and what it still shows

Hide apps on Home and Apps screens is the setting Samsung Galaxy phones use to take an app's icon out of sight without uninstalling it. This guide gives the exact steps, how to bring an app back, and the places a hidden app still shows up, so you know what it really protects.

Quick answer

On a Samsung Galaxy, touch and hold an empty spot on the Home screen, tap Settings, then tap Hide apps on Home and Apps screens. Tap the apps you want to hide and tap Done. To unhide one, go back to the same screen, tap the red minus on it and tap Done. Hidden apps can still be found through search and in Settings, and they are not locked, so use Secure Folder or an encrypted vault for anything that must stay private.

How to hide apps on Home and Apps screens (step by step)

These steps follow Samsung's own support pages. Samsung's Australian page, updated in August 2026, labels the option just Hide apps; the US page and most phones say Hide apps on Home and Apps screens. They are the same setting.

  1. Touch and hold an empty spot on the Home screen. Pick a space with no icon or widget. The screen zooms out and shows options along the bottom.
  2. Tap Settings. This opens the Home screen settings, which belong to Samsung's launcher, One UI Home. It is not the main Settings app.
  3. Tap Hide apps on Home and Apps screens. You will see a grid of your apps, with any already hidden shown in their own list.
  4. Tap each app you want to hide. Each one you tap moves into the hidden list.
  5. Tap Done. The icons disappear from both the Home screen and the Apps screen. The apps are still installed and keep all their data.

If you do not see the option, check that Samsung's launcher is your default: open Settings, Apps, choose default apps and look at Home app. Hide apps is a feature of One UI Home and does not exist in other launchers.

How to unhide apps on Samsung

  1. Touch and hold the Home screen and tap Settings.
  2. Tap Hide apps on Home and Apps screens.
  3. In the list of hidden apps, tap the red minus on the app.
  4. Tap Done. The app returns to the Apps screen. Add it to the Home screen again by holding its icon and choosing Add to Home.

Need to open a hidden app once without unhiding it? Samsung's support page says to type its name into the search bar on the Apps screen. That is handy for you, and it is also the first limit to understand.

Hide apps without changing your home screen

Many people only want an icon gone from the Home screen, not from the whole phone. For that, you do not need the Hide apps setting at all:

  1. Touch and hold the app's icon on the Home screen.
  2. Tap Remove, not Uninstall.

Only the shortcut goes. The app stays installed and stays in the Apps screen, and the rest of your Home screen layout is left alone. It hides nothing from anyone who opens the Apps screen, but it keeps a shared or handed-over Home screen tidy.

If you want an app hidden everywhere but you are worried about your layout, hide it with the steps above and check your Home screen afterwards. Other icons are not deleted; only the hidden app's shortcut is taken away.

Why hiding an app is not the same as protecting it

Hide apps on Home and Apps screens is a tidying tool. It changes what the launcher draws, and nothing else. Here is what a curious person can still do in under a minute:

  • Search for it. The Apps screen search finds hidden apps by name, and Samsung documents this as the way to open them.
  • Open Settings, Apps. Every installed app is listed there by its real name, hidden or not.
  • Check the Play Store. The Manage apps list shows what is installed on the phone.
  • Undo it. The hidden list is two taps away from the Home screen, with no password.
  • See its notifications. Hiding the icon does not hide what the app sends to your notification panel. Turn those off in the app's notification settings if that matters.

And when the app is opened, everything inside it is exactly as it was. A hidden gallery or notes app shows its contents to whoever finds it. Most people who look through a phone are partners, family or friends who already know the screen lock, so a hidden icon slows them down a little and no more. If that has already happened to you, see what to do if someone went through your phone.

When to use Secure Folder instead

If you need an app to be hidden and locked, Samsung's stronger option is Secure Folder. It keeps separate copies of apps and their data behind their own lock, which can be different from your phone's PIN.

  1. Set it up from Settings, Security and privacy, More security settings, Secure Folder, and choose a lock you do not use anywhere else.
  2. Inside Secure Folder, tap + to add apps. The copy inside has its own data, separate from the one outside.
  3. Optional: in Secure Folder's settings, turn off Add Secure Folder to Apps screen. You then open it from its Quick Settings tile.

Remember that the original app is still on the main side unless you remove it there. Secure Folder is tied to your Galaxy and its software, and it does not exist on other brands. For a closer look at its strengths and limits, see Sealcloak vs Samsung Secure Folder.

Often it is the photos you want hidden, not the app

A lot of people reach for Hide apps because they want to hide a gallery, a vault or a chat app. If the real worry is private photos, videos or documents, hide those directly. Samsung's own options for pictures are covered in Samsung hidden photos.

Sealcloak is one other route, and it works the same on every Galaxy with Android 8.0 or newer. It cannot hide other apps. What it does instead:

  • It disguises itself. With the calculator disguise (Pro), the app becomes a working calculator, and the vault opens only when you type your secret code of 4 to 8 digits and hold the equals key for 2 seconds. A custom icon (Pro) lets you pick any picture and name. There is no hidden app to search for.
  • It encrypts what is inside. Every file is encrypted on the phone with AES-256-GCM, and it opens with its own pattern on a 5×5 grid, not your phone PIN. Fingerprint unlock is optional.
  • It has no account and no internet permission, so it cannot upload anything. Screenshots and screen recording are blocked inside it.
  • It shows you failed attempts. Optional intruder photos take a front camera picture from the 3rd wrong pattern in a row.

Adding files offers to keep the originals, so delete them from Samsung Gallery yourself afterwards and empty its Recycle bin. The details are on the security page and the full feature list.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Treating a hidden app as locked. Search opens it with no password.
  • Tapping Uninstall instead of Remove. On the Home screen menu, Remove only takes away the shortcut; Uninstall deletes the app and its data.
  • Forgetting notifications. A message preview on the lock screen gives the app away faster than any icon.
  • Hiding a gallery but not its files. The photos are still in Samsung Gallery, Google Photos and any chat app that received them.
  • Using your phone PIN for Secure Folder. It works, but it lets in exactly the people who already know your PIN.
  • Switching launchers and losing track. Apps hidden in One UI Home appear again in another launcher, and its own hidden list is separate.

Bottom line

Hide apps on Home and Apps screens is the quickest way to clear an icon off a Samsung phone: hold the Home screen, tap Settings, pick the apps, tap Done. It keeps a phone tidy and stops a quick glance, but it does not lock anything, and search still finds the app. For real privacy on a Galaxy, put apps in Secure Folder with its own lock, and keep private photos and files somewhere encrypted.
