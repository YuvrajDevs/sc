How to hide apps on Android

Sometimes it isn't only the photos you want private, it's the fact that an app is on your phone at all. Android gives you several ways to hide apps, from removing an icon to a whole separate locked space. Here's how each works on popular phones, and what it still leaves visible.

Quick answer

To hide an app on Android, use your launcher's Hide apps option (on Samsung: Home screen settings, Hide apps), or move it into Private space on Android 15 and newer, or Secure Folder on Galaxy phones. Hidden apps still appear in Settings, so for a private vault, a disguised icon plus real encryption works best.

1. Hide apps in your launcher

Most launchers can remove an app from the home screen and app drawer without uninstalling it.

  1. Samsung: long-press the home screen, tap Settings, then Hide apps on Home and Apps screens, and select the apps.
  2. Xiaomi, Redmi, POCO: look under Settings, Apps, App lock, Hidden apps, or in the home screen settings, depending on your version.
  3. OPPO, realme, OnePlus: search Settings for "Hide apps"; some versions use a dial-code to open them.
  4. Pixel: the stock launcher doesn't hide apps; use Private space instead.

Watch out: the app still appears in Settings, Apps, and in the Play Store's installed list, and anyone who knows the menu can unhide it.

2. Android Private space

On Android 15 and newer, Settings, Security and privacy, Private space creates a locked area at the bottom of the app drawer. Apps installed inside are hidden and paused until you unlock it, and their notifications don't show while it's locked. On some phones you can hide the Private space entry itself, so it only appears when you search for it.

Watch out: it's only on newer phones and depends on your phone maker. Compare it with a vault in Private space vs Sealcloak.

3. Samsung Secure Folder

Secure Folder keeps separate copies of apps and their data behind their own lock, and you can rename its icon or hide it. It's one of the strongest built-in options on Galaxy phones. More in Secure Folder compared.

4. Disguise the app as something else

Some vault apps can change their own icon and name, so on your home screen they look like an ordinary app. Opened normally, they show nothing unusual; only your code or pattern reveals the vault. This works on any Android phone, and it's the hardest for a curious person to notice, because there's nothing "hidden" to look for.

What makes a good disguise:

  • A working front. A calculator that really calculates is far more convincing than a blank screen.
  • Your choice of icon and name, so it blends in with apps you already have.
  • Real encryption underneath. The disguise hides that a vault exists; encryption protects the files if someone finds it.
  • No account and no internet, or the "hidden" app is still talking to a server about you.

How Sealcloak hides itself

Sealcloak offers two disguises. The calculator disguise turns the app into a working calculator; you open the vault by typing your secret code of 4 to 8 digits and holding the equals key for two seconds. A custom icon lets you pick any picture and name. Either way, the vault behind it is encrypted with AES-256-GCM, screenshots are blocked so recent apps shows nothing, and the app has no internet permission. See the security details.

The honest limit

Android's Settings, Apps list shows every installed app by its real name, and the Play Store lists what you've installed. No method makes an app truly invisible to someone who goes looking. That's why the contents matter more than the icon: a hidden app with unencrypted files is still exposed; a disguised vault with encrypted files stays private even if someone finds it. Hiding apps from children

If the goal is stopping children opening certain apps on a phone you share, hiding isn't the best tool; they find menus quickly. App pinning keeps the phone locked to one app until you unlock it, and a kids mode or a separate device managed with Google Family Link gives them their own space. Our page on kids using your phone walks through each option.

Hiding apps before handing over your phone

Repairs, borrowed phones and checks at work or school are the moments people most often search for this. Hiding icons helps a little; a repair or maintenance mode helps more, because it hides your whole account. See giving your phone for repair.

How to find hidden apps

If you're checking your own phone for apps someone else installed, open Settings, Apps and scroll the full list, including system apps. Look for apps with device administrator access (Settings, Security) or accessibility access you didn't grant, and run a Play Protect scan from the Play Store.
