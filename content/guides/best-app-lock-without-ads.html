Best app lock for Android without ads: the built-in locks first

You want an app lock that keeps WhatsApp, your gallery and your banking app shut, without a full-screen ad every time you unlock one. The best app lock for Android without ads is often already on your phone. This guide shows where each brand hides it, how to check a Play Store app lock before installing it, and where any lock stops protecting you.

Quick answer

Start with the lock your phone already has, because it shows no ads at all: App lock on Xiaomi, Redmi, POCO, realme and OnePlus, Privacy and app encryption on vivo, Secure Folder on Samsung and Motorola, and Private space on Android 15 and newer. Open Settings and search for those names. Only if your phone has none, pick a Play Store app lock without the Contains ads label and with few permissions, and keep private photos in an encrypted vault, since an app lock never encrypts the files.

Why ads and app locks are a bad mix

An app lock sits between you and your most private apps. You see its screen every time you open a locked chat or your gallery, which can be 30 or 40 times a day. For an app that earns money from ads, that is prime space, and many free locks fill it with banners, full-screen ads after unlocking, or "boost" and "clean" pop-ups that have nothing to do with locking.

The annoyance is the smaller problem. To show ads, an app needs internet access and usually an advertising library that talks to outside servers. At the same time, an app lock needs strong permissions to do its job: it has to know which app you just opened, and it has to draw its lock screen over other apps. Some ask for Accessibility access, which lets an app read what is on screen. You are giving those powers to an app whose business is showing you ads. That is a reason to be choosy, not to panic, but it is why "without ads" is a sensible thing to search for.

The good news: the lock built into your phone has none of these issues. It comes with the system, it gets updated with it, and it doesn't show ads.

Step-by-step: set up a built-in app lock properly

The details differ by brand, but these steps work on nearly every Android phone that has a lock built in.

  1. Search Settings. Type App lock. If nothing appears, try App encryption, Secure folder or Private space.
  2. Set the code the feature asks for. Many brands use a privacy password that is separate from your screen lock. Make it different from your lock screen PIN, and add the recovery option offered, such as a security question or your account.
  3. Lock the right apps. Chat apps, Photos or Gallery, Files, email, banking and payment apps. Lock Settings and Play Store too if your phone allows it, so nobody can switch the lock off or uninstall things.
  4. Choose to relock on exit. "Lock when the screen locks" leaves an app open for whoever you hand the phone to next. Locking as soon as you leave the app is tighter.
  5. Hide notification content. In Settings, then Notifications, set locked apps to hide message previews. Otherwise new messages show on the lock screen whatever the app lock says.
  6. Test it. Clear the app from Recents, turn the screen off and on, then open it. The lock prompt should appear before any content does.

If you install an app lock, how to pick one without ads

Some phones, especially older Samsung models and some Android One devices, have no per-app lock. If you need one from the Play Store, run through these checks on its listing before you tap Install.

  • The Contains ads label. It sits under the app's name and install button. Google requires every developer to declare whether the app shows ads, and Google may add the label itself if it finds ads. No label is a good sign. "In-app purchases" alone is different: it means you can pay for something, not that you will see ads.
  • The Data safety section. Scroll down and open it. Look at what the app says it collects and shares. An app lock has no need to share your app activity or device IDs with anyone.
  • The permissions it asks for. Usage access and "display over other apps" are normal for an app lock. Accessibility access is more powerful; only grant it to an app you trust. Contacts, location or call logs have nothing to do with locking apps.
  • Recent reviews. Sort by most recent and look for complaints about new ads after an update, a paid "remove ads" pop-up, or the lock being easy to skip.
  • A "remove ads" upgrade. Paying to remove ads is fine, but check what still happens in the background. The ad library is often still part of the app.

What no app lock does: protect the photos themselves

This is the part most "best app lock" lists skip. An app lock guards the door of an app, not the files behind it. Lock your gallery, and your pictures still sit unencrypted in the phone's shared storage. A file manager, any app you gave photo access, a computer over USB, or a Google Photos backup on another device can still show them. And if someone learns your app lock code, every locked app opens at once.

For apps, the built-in lock is the right tool. For private photos, videos and documents, the stronger move is to take them out of the gallery entirely and keep them somewhere encrypted. Our guide on how to hide photos on Android explains the difference between hiding, locking and encrypting.

That is the job Sealcloak does. It is an offline vault for Android 8.0 and newer, and it has no ads, no account, no analytics and no internet permission at all, so nothing in it can be uploaded. Files you add are encrypted on the phone with AES-256-GCM, so the vault folder is unreadable from a file manager or a laptop. You open it with a pattern of at least 6 dots on a 5x5 grid, with fingerprint or face unlock as an option, and each vault gets a 9-word recovery phrase in case you forget the pattern. Turn on intruder photos and the front camera takes a picture of anyone who gets the pattern wrong three times in a row. More detail is on the security page.

To be clear about its limits: Sealcloak can't lock or hide other apps such as WhatsApp. It can only disguise its own icon. So pair the two: your phone's built-in app lock for apps, and the vault for the files that matter. When you add photos, Sealcloak asks whether to keep the originals, and deleting them from the gallery is up to you.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Reusing your screen lock PIN. Anyone who has watched you unlock the phone can then open every locked app.
  • Locking Photos but not Files. The same pictures are usually a tap away in the file manager.
  • Installing two locks. A third-party lock on top of a built-in one adds permissions and ads without adding protection.
  • Ignoring notifications. Message previews on the lock screen bypass any app lock.
  • Trusting "unlock tools" when you forget the code. Use the maker's reset option. Sideloaded unlock files and clearing an app's storage can cost you your data.

Bottom line

The best app lock for Android without ads is usually the one your phone already has: App lock on Xiaomi, realme and OnePlus, Privacy and app encryption on vivo, Secure Folder on Samsung and Motorola, and Private space on Android 15 and newer. It costs nothing and shows no ads. If you must install one, check for the Contains ads label, read the Data safety section and refuse permissions it doesn't need. Then remember what a lock is: a door, not a safe. Keep truly private photos and documents in an encrypted offline vault, and let the app lock handle your apps.
