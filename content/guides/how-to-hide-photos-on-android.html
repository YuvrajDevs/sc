How to hide photos on Android

There are six common ways to hide photos on an Android phone. Some only tidy your gallery; others genuinely lock photos away. The right one depends on one question: who are you hiding them from? Here's what each method does, how to use it, and where it falls short.

Quick answer

To hide photos on Android quickly, move them to Google Photos' Locked Folder or your gallery's hidden album. If someone around you knows your phone's screen lock, use an encrypted vault app with its own separate lock, such as Sealcloak, then delete the originals and empty the gallery's trash.

1. Archive in Google Photos

Select the photos, tap the three-dot menu and choose Archive. They leave your main photo grid but stay in the Archive section and still show up in search.

Use it for: tidying screenshots and receipts. Don't use it for: anything private. Anyone who opens Google Photos can find archived photos in seconds.

2. Your gallery's hidden album

Samsung Gallery, Xiaomi Gallery, OPPO, vivo and realme galleries can hide an album or move photos to a hidden or private album. On some phones it needs a password; on others it's simply tucked into a menu.

Use it for: keeping a few photos off the main screen. Watch out: the option is usually one tap away in the gallery's menu, and the files can often still be seen in a file manager or on a computer.

3. Google Photos Locked Folder

  1. Open Google Photos and go to Collections (or Library), then Locked Folder.
  2. Set it up and confirm with your screen lock.
  3. Select photos elsewhere in Google Photos, tap the menu and choose Move to Locked Folder.

Photos in Locked Folder don't appear in your grid, albums, search or memories, and other apps can't see them. Watch out: it opens with your phone's own screen lock, so anyone who knows it can get in. Read our Locked Folder comparison for details.

4. Samsung Secure Folder

On Galaxy phones, open Settings, then Security and privacy, and look for Secure Folder (the exact menu varies by model). It creates a separate space with its own lock, gallery and apps, and you can move photos into it from the Gallery app.

Use it for: Galaxy owners who want a whole separate space. Watch out: it's only on Samsung phones, and it doesn't offer a decoy or show who tried to get in. See Secure Folder compared.

5. Android Private space

Android 15 and newer can create a Private space under Settings, Security and privacy. It's a hidden area for apps; install a gallery or file app inside it to keep photos there.

Use it for: hiding entire apps. Watch out: it's only on newer phones and it's built for apps, so keeping individual photos inside it is fiddly. More in Private space compared.

6. An encrypted vault app

A vault app moves photos into its own encrypted storage, behind a lock that's separate from your phone's. Once a copy is safely inside, you delete the original from the gallery. This is the only method here that protects photos even from someone who knows your screen lock.

Not all vault apps are equal. Before you choose one, check:

  • Is it really encrypted? Some vaults only hide files in a folder. Look for named encryption such as AES-256.
  • Does it need an account or the internet? An account or cloud sync usually means your photos, or data about you, leave the phone. Check the Play Store's data safety section.
  • Can you get back in? There should be a recovery method that doesn't depend on a company.
  • Can you back it up? Without a backup, a lost phone means lost photos.

Which method should you use?

  • Just tidying up: Archive.
  • Hiding from a quick glance, and nobody knows your screen lock: Locked Folder, a hidden album or Secure Folder.
  • Someone close knows your lock, or the photos are sensitive: an encrypted vault with its own pattern.
  • Hiding whole apps: Private space or Secure Folder, plus a vault for the files.

Don't forget the copies

Hiding a photo in one place doesn't remove it everywhere else. After moving private photos, check:

  1. The gallery's trash, which keeps deleted photos for up to 30 days.
  2. Google Photos or other cloud backups: delete the copy there too.
  3. WhatsApp and other chat folders, which keep every photo you've received.
  4. Downloads and Screenshots folders.

And before sharing any photo, consider removing its hidden location.
