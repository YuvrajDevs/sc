Photo vault not working or not opening: safe fixes

Your photo vault is not working. Maybe it crashes on launch, sits on a blank screen, says your photos can't load, or opens to an empty album. It's frightening, because the things inside are the ones you care about most. The good news is that most of these problems are fixable, and the files are usually still there. This guide gives the fixes in a safe order, so nothing you try makes it worse.

Quick answer

First, don't uninstall the vault or tap Clear storage (Clear data), because in most vault apps that erases the hidden files. Restart the phone, make sure it has at least 1 to 2 GB free, then force stop the app and clear only its cache. Update the app, check its permissions, and turn off Pause app activity if unused for it. If it still won't open, check the memory card or account sign-in, and write to the developer before trying anything else.

The one rule: never tap Clear storage or uninstall

Almost every guide for a broken app says "clear data and reinstall". For a normal app that's fine. For a vault, it's often the worst thing you can do.

Vault apps store their files, their lock code and their encryption keys in the app's private storage. When you tap Clear storage (some phones call it Clear data) or uninstall the app, Android deletes that private storage. You'd come back to a fresh, empty vault asking you to create a new code. Some apps keep a copy in the cloud or on the memory card, but many don't, and you can't easily tell which kind you have when it won't open.

Clear cache is different. The cache only holds temporary files such as thumbnails, and clearing it is normally harmless. Keep that difference in mind for every step below.

Step-by-step: fix a photo vault that is not opening

Work down this list in order and test the vault after each step. Most problems are solved by step 3.

  1. Restart the phone. Hold the power button and choose Restart. A stuck background process or a half-finished update is the most common reason an app freezes on its first screen.
  2. Check free space. Open Settings and search for Storage. If the phone is nearly full, apps can crash while trying to load or decrypt large files. Free at least 1 to 2 GB by clearing downloads or old videos you've already backed up.
  3. Force stop the app and clear its cache. Touch and hold the vault's icon, tap App info, then Force stop. Next open Storage & cache (just Storage on some phones) and tap Clear cache. Do not tap Clear storage. Open the vault again.
  4. Update the app. Open Google Play, search for the vault and tap Update if it's offered. Crashes after an Android update are often fixed by the developer within days. If the app updated itself just before the problem started, check its Play Store reviews to see if others have the same issue.
  5. Check its permissions. In App info, open Permissions. Vault apps may need Photos and videos, Files and media, or All files access to move files in and out. If a permission shows as Not allowed, turn it back on.
  6. Turn off Pause app activity if unused. If you rarely open the vault, Android may have removed its permissions and stopped it in the background. In App info, find Pause app activity if unused (on older versions, Remove permissions if app isn't used) and turn it off. Google's help pages describe this feature under managing unused apps.
  7. Check battery restrictions. Some phones put rarely used apps into deep sleep. In App info, open Battery or App battery usage and set the vault to unrestricted, at least while you fix it. On Samsung phones also check that it isn't in the Deep sleeping apps list.
  8. Check the memory card. If the vault stored files on an SD card, make sure the card is in, shows up under Storage, and is the same card. Many vaults simply show an empty album when their card is missing.
  9. Contact the developer. The Play Store listing has a developer email under App support. Include your phone model, Android version, app version and exactly what you see. Ask them how to back up before you try anything drastic.

When a photo vault is not syncing

Vaults that keep a cloud copy can show "not syncing", "backup failed" or a spinning cloud icon. In most cases the photos on the phone are untouched. The usual causes are simple:

  • You were signed out. A password change or a new phone can end the session. Sign back in with the same email you used at setup.
  • The connection is blocked. Some apps only sync on Wi-Fi by default, and data saver or a VPN can stop them.
  • The cloud quota is full. Free plans often have a small limit, and sync just stops when it's reached.
  • The service is down or closing. Check the app's site or recent Play Store reviews. If the service is shutting down, export your files while the app still opens.

Whatever you do, don't sign out, uninstall or switch accounts to "refresh" a vault that isn't syncing. If the cloud copy is incomplete, the local copy may be the only full set you have.

Is a photo vault safe or not?

Many people searching for a broken vault are really asking a bigger question: should I trust this app with my photos at all? A failure is a good moment to check. Look at four things on its Google Play listing and its own site:

  • Encryption. Does it encrypt the files, or only hide them in a folder with a dot in front of the name? Hidden-only files can be found by any file manager or computer.
  • Accounts and uploads. The data safety section shows what's collected and shared. An account and a cloud copy mean another company holds your private photos.
  • Recovery. What happens if you forget the code? Email resets are convenient but mean someone else can let a person in. No recovery at all means one forgotten code loses everything.
  • Backup. Can you make a copy that survives a broken app, a lost phone or a reset? If not, a crash like the one you're fixing could cost you every file.

Mistakes to avoid while it's broken

  • Reinstalling "to fix it". In most vaults this deletes the very files you're trying to reach.
  • Factory resetting the phone. It wipes every app's private storage. Only consider it after you have a working backup.
  • Downloading "vault recovery" tools. They can't open properly encrypted files, and many ask for payment or broad permissions first.
  • Moving the SD card to another phone. Vault files on a card are often tied to the original phone and app. Put it back where it was.
  • Waiting to make a backup. As soon as the vault opens again, export or back up its contents somewhere other than the phone.

Bottom line

When a photo vault is not working, the files are usually still on the phone. Go slowly: restart, free up space, force stop, clear cache only, update, and check permissions and the unused-apps setting. Never tap Clear storage or uninstall while you hope to get your photos back. If it's a sync problem, sign back in rather than signing out. And once it works again, make a backup you control, so the next crash is an inconvenience rather than a loss.
