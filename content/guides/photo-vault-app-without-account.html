Photo vault app without an account

Most photo vault apps want something before they protect anything: an email, a phone number, or a sign-in for their cloud. That account is another way into your private photos, and another place data about you lives. Here's why a vault without an account is safer, and how to check any app before you trust it.

Quick answer

A photo vault without an account keeps your photos and your identity off anyone's servers. Look for one that needs no sign-in, has no internet permission, encrypts files on the phone, and gives you a recovery phrase and backups you hold yourself. Sealcloak works this way.

Why an account is a risk

  • Breaches. Accounts are stored on servers. When those leak, emails and passwords leak with them, and reused passwords open other doors.
  • Account takeover. If someone gets into your email, they may reset your vault account too.
  • Cloud copies. Many accounts exist to sync or back up photos online. A copy on a server is a copy you can't fully control.
  • Tracking. An account ties your use of the app to your identity, often alongside analytics and advertising IDs.
  • Pressure. Anything a company holds can be requested from it, or exposed if the company changes hands.

How to check any vault app in two minutes

  1. Open the app's page on Google Play and scroll to Data safety. Look at "Data shared" and "Data collected". Photos, videos, email, location or device IDs listed there tell you what leaves the phone.
  2. Read the listing for words like cloud, sync, backup to our servers, sign in.
  3. Check whether the app needs internet access. A vault that can't connect can't upload.
  4. Find out how you recover if you forget your code. Email reset means an account; a phrase you keep means you're in control.
  5. Check how backups work, and whether they're encrypted.

How Sealcloak works without an account

Sealcloak never asks for an email, phone number or sign-in, and it doesn't have Android's internet permission, so it can't upload anything even by mistake. Every file is encrypted with AES-256-GCM on your phone, using a key made from your pattern with Argon2id. The pattern itself is never stored.

Without an account, recovery comes from you: each vault gets a 9-word recovery phrase when you create it. Write it on paper. It's the only spare key, and nobody else, including us, has a copy. To protect against a lost phone, encrypted backups run by hand or every week or month to a folder you pick, and restore on any Android phone with your phrase. More in how it works.

The trade-off, honestly

No account means no company can rescue you. If you lose both your pattern and your recovery phrase and have no backup, nobody can open that vault. That's the same property that keeps everyone else out. Keep your phrase somewhere safe, away from the phone, and let scheduled backups run, and an offline vault is both private and dependable.

What "no internet" really means

Android apps have to declare the permissions they use, and internet access is one of them. An app without the internet permission can't open a network connection: no uploads, no analytics, no ads, no "anonymous" usage pings. That's a stronger guarantee than a privacy policy, because the operating system enforces it rather than the developer promising it.

You can see an app's permissions on its Google Play page under "About this app", then "App permissions", or on your phone in Settings, Apps, then the app, then Permissions. A vault that lists network access can talk to the internet; one that doesn't, can't.

Accounts and changing phones

The usual argument for an account is moving to a new phone. An offline vault handles that with a file instead of a login. Make an encrypted backup on the old phone, copy it across by cable, memory card or computer, and restore it on the new phone with your recovery phrase. The backup is encrypted, so it's safe to carry, and nothing ever passes through a company's servers. If you're selling the old phone afterwards, our guide on deleting photos before selling covers the rest.

Setting up an account-free vault

  1. Install Sealcloak and draw a pattern of at least six dots, different from your screen lock.
  2. Write down the 9-word recovery phrase on paper.
  3. Add your private photos, then delete the originals from the gallery and empty its trash.
  4. Turn on scheduled backups and copy a backup to a computer now and then.
