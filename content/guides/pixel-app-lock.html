Pixel app lock: how to lock and hide apps on a Google Pixel

Samsung, Xiaomi and vivo phones have an app lock menu with a switch next to every app. If you've searched your Google Pixel for the same thing, you probably came up empty. A Pixel app lock does exist, but it works differently, and a true per-app lock is still in testing. This guide shows what works on a Pixel today, step by step, and where each option stops.

Quick answer

On a Pixel with Android 15 or newer, the built-in app lock is Private space. Open Settings, tap Security & privacy, then Private space, tap Set up and choose a separate lock. Install the apps you want locked inside it, uninstall the normal copies, and turn on Hide private space when it's locked to hide them too. Google's per-app App lock is in beta and expected for everyone in early 2027.

Is there an app lock on Google Pixel?

Short answer: there's a locked area, not a lock switch per app. Here is where things stand in November 2026.

  • Private space (available now). Added in Android 15. It's a separate, locked part of the phone with its own apps. When it's locked, those apps are stopped and, if you choose, hidden from the app list entirely. This is the Pixel's answer to both "app lock" and "hide apps".
  • App lock (in beta). Google is testing a native App lock that locks individual apps with your fingerprint, face, PIN, pattern or password. Neowin reported on 19 November 2026 that it is in the Android 17 QPR3 Beta 3 for Pixel, with a wider release planned for the first quarter of 2027. In the beta you long-press an app icon and pick App lock, and manage the list in Settings, Security & privacy, App lock.
  • No hide option in the Pixel Launcher. Unlike many other launchers, the stock Pixel home screen can't hide a single icon. Android 17 lets you hide app names on the home screen, but the icons stay.

If you aren't on the beta, don't wait for App lock. Private space does the job now, and it protects more than a simple lock screen would.

Step-by-step: set up Private space on a Pixel

These steps follow Google's own help page. You need Android 15 or later, you must be the phone's main user, and it won't work on a work-managed phone or one with more than four users or profiles.

  1. Open Settings and tap Security & privacy.
  2. Under Privacy, tap Private space. Confirm with your screen lock. If you don't have one, the phone asks you to create it first.
  3. Tap Set up, then Got it.
  4. Decide on a Google account. You can sign in to one, and Google recommends a separate account that isn't used elsewhere on the phone. This keeps Play Store history and backups for the private apps apart from your main account.
  5. Choose a lock. Tap Choose new lock and set a PIN, pattern or password that's different from your screen lock. Use device screen lock is quicker, but then anyone who knows your phone PIN can open the space too.
  6. Tap Done. Private space now appears at the bottom of All Apps.
  7. Add apps. Open Private space, tap Install, and get the apps you want from the Play Store inside the space. Sign in to them again. They are separate copies with their own data.
  8. Uninstall the outside copies. If WhatsApp is inside Private space but also on your normal home screen, anyone can open the normal one.

How to hide apps on a Google Pixel

Locking keeps people out. Hiding stops them from knowing there's anything to open. Private space does both:

  1. Scroll to Private space in All Apps and tap it. Unlock it.
  2. Tap the Settings icon on the right of Private space.
  3. Under Hide, turn on Hide private space when it's locked.
  4. Lock the space. It disappears from All Apps the next time it locks, not straight away.

To get back in, type "private space" into the search bar at the top of All Apps and enter your lock. In the same settings screen, Lock private space automatically lets you choose Every time device locks, 5 minutes after screen timeout, or Only after the device restarts. Every time device locks is the safest choice. For the same job on other brands, see our guide to hiding apps on Android.

What a Pixel app lock doesn't protect (the part most guides skip)

Both tools answer the question "who can open this app?" Neither answers "who can see my pictures?", and that's usually what people actually worry about.

  • Your gallery stays where it is. Putting a gallery app in Private space doesn't move your pictures. Photos in the normal storage are still visible in Google Photos, Files, any app with photo access and a computer on USB.
  • App lock isn't isolation. Google's own description of the beta says other apps that already have access to a locked app's data can still reach it. An app with photo access can read photos even if Photos is locked.
  • No recovery. Forget the Private space lock and the only way out is deleting the space. Google's help adds that its on-device data isn't included in device backups, so it won't come back after a reset or on a new phone.
  • Notifications pause when locked. That's a privacy plus, but if a chat app is inside, you won't see new messages until you unlock the space.
  • Cloud copies are separate. If Google Photos backup is on, your pictures are also on every device signed in to that account.

So treat Private space as a lock for apps, and give your pictures their own protection. Our guide on how to hide photos on Android explains the difference between hiding, locking and encrypting.

Locking photos and files on a Pixel, not just apps

Pixels use Google Photos as the gallery, and its Locked Folder is the free built-in option. Photos you move there vanish from the main grid and from other apps and open with your screen lock. It's fine for a few pictures, but anyone who knows your PIN can open it. Our gallery lock guide covers it in more detail.

If you want the files themselves encrypted behind a separate lock, Sealcloak is one option built for that. It's an offline vault for photos, videos, documents, audio, notes and scans, and it runs on any Pixel with Android 8.0 or newer.

  • Nothing leaves the phone. You don't create an account or give an email. Sealcloak has no internet permission at all, so your files can't be uploaded, synced or analysed.
  • Encrypted files, not just a locked door. Each item is scrambled with AES-256-GCM before it's stored. Plug the Pixel into a laptop and browse its storage, and the vault is unreadable. Our security page covers the key handling.
  • A lock nobody else knows. You draw a pattern on a 5x5 grid, six dots or more, that has nothing to do with your Pixel PIN. Fingerprint or face unlock can be added on top.
  • Recovery that Private space lacks. Each vault comes with a 9-word recovery phrase. Only you hold it, which also means a lost pattern plus a lost phrase can't be undone by anyone.
  • Hidden in plain sight. With Pro, calculator disguise turns the app into a working calculator, which helps since the Pixel Launcher can't hide icons.

Where it falls short: Sealcloak only disguises itself and can't lock or hide WhatsApp or any other app, so Private space still has a job. It also won't pull photos out of Google Photos on its own. You choose what to import, it asks whether to keep the originals, and removing them from the gallery is up to you. Use the two together: Private space for apps, the vault for pictures and documents.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Reusing your Pixel PIN. Someone who has glanced at your screen while you unlocked it can then open Private space as well.
  • Forgetting the normal copy of an app. If WhatsApp is still on the home screen, locking the private copy achieves nothing.
  • Choosing Only after the device restarts. The space then stays open all day once you've unlocked it.
  • Keeping the only copy of important data inside. Private space isn't in device backups, and a forgotten lock means deleting it.
  • Installing "Pixel hide app" mods. Guides that add a hide option to the Pixel Launcher need root, which weakens the phone's security far more than a visible icon does.
  • Thinking a hidden gallery app hides the gallery. The pictures still sit in shared storage. Move them into Locked Folder or a vault.

Bottom line

The Pixel app lock you can use today is Private space. Find it in Settings, Security & privacy, Private space. Give it its own lock, install the apps inside, uninstall the outside copies, and turn on Hide private space when it's locked if you don't want it noticed. Set it to lock every time the phone locks. Google's per-app App lock is coming, likely in early 2027, but it won't isolate data the way Private space does. Neither one protects photos in your gallery, so pair it with Google Photos Locked Folder or an encrypted offline vault.
