How to hide videos on Android, with or without an app

Videos are harder to hide than photos. They're big, they autoplay in previews, and chat apps and cloud backups quietly keep extra copies. This guide shows every practical way to hide videos on Android, what each one protects, and how to clean up the copies that give a hidden video away.

Quick answer

To hide videos on Android without an app, select them in Google Photos and tap Move to Locked Folder, or use Files by Google Safe folder, Samsung's Private album or Secure Folder, or Private space on Android 15 and newer. If you need the video encrypted behind a lock that's separate from your screen lock, move it into an offline vault app instead. Either way, delete the copies in chat folders, downloads and cloud trash, or the video isn't really hidden.

Before you start: find every copy of the video

A photo usually lives in one place. A video often lives in three or four. Hiding the one you can see in your gallery does nothing about the rest, and this is the most common reason people say "I hid it, but it's still there".

  • The camera folder. Anything you recorded sits in DCIM/Camera, which every gallery and file manager can read.
  • Chat app folders. A video you sent or received on WhatsApp, Telegram or similar is usually saved again in that app's own media folder.
  • Edited and trimmed versions. Trimming or adding a filter in most gallery apps saves a new file and keeps the original.
  • Downloads and screen recordings. These go to their own folders and are easy to forget.
  • Cloud backup. If Google Photos, OneDrive or a phone maker's cloud backs up your camera roll, the video is also on a server and on any device signed into that account.

Open your file manager and search for the date the video was made or for "mp4". It takes two minutes and tells you exactly what you need to deal with.

How to hide videos on Android without an app

These are the built-in or pre-installed options. All of them are free, and for many people one of them is enough.

Google Photos Locked Folder

Works on most Android phones that have Google Photos. Google's own help page says Locked Folder covers videos as well as photos.

  1. Open Google Photos and select the videos. Long-press one, then tap any others.
  2. Tap More, then Move to Locked Folder. The first time, Google Photos walks you through setting it up.
  3. Confirm with Move. Keep the app open until long videos finish moving.

Two details from Google's help page matter here. Related items, such as copies, edited versions and videos others shared with you, are not moved with it. And if backup for Locked Folder is off, Google deletes the cloud backups of anything you move in. That's good for privacy, but it means the only copy is now on your phone.

Files by Google Safe folder

Files by Google is pre-installed on many phones and free on Google Play for Android 8.0 and up. Its Safe folder is protected by a PIN or pattern you choose, which is a real advantage: it isn't your screen lock. Long-press a video in Files, tap the three-dot menu and choose Move to Safe folder. Google is blunt about the catch: if you forget the PIN or pattern, you can't get the files back, and resetting the Safe folder deletes what's inside.

Samsung Galaxy: Private album or Secure Folder

Samsung's support pages describe two options. On select Galaxy phones with One UI 8.5, the Gallery has a Private album: select the videos, tap More, then Move to private album. Items in it sit behind your device's security lock. On older and other Galaxy phones, use Secure Folder: select the videos in Gallery, tap More and pick Move to Secure Folder. Samsung notes that Copy leaves the original in place, so choose Move. Our Samsung hidden photos guide covers both in more depth.

Samsung also lets you hide whole albums from the Albums view through the three-dot menu and Hide albums. That only removes them from view. It doesn't lock anything.

Hidden albums on Xiaomi, vivo, OPPO and others

Most other brands' gallery apps have a hidden or private album, and some have a file safe in their file manager. Menu names change between versions, so search your Settings or gallery menu for "hidden", "private" or "safe". These usually open with a separate privacy password or your screen lock. Our vivo app lock guide shows how File safe works on vivo as one example.

Android Private space (Android 15 and newer)

Private space is a separate, lockable area for apps. Google's help page puts it under Settings, then Security & privacy, then Private space. You can install a gallery or file app inside it and keep videos there. Google recommends giving it its own lock rather than reusing the screen lock. It's a heavier tool than the others and only exists on Android 15 or later.

Why the lock matters more than the hiding

Every method above hides the video. The real question is who can still open it. Think about who you're hiding it from.

  • A child or a friend borrowing the phone for a minute: archive or a hidden album is usually fine. They won't go looking.
  • Someone who knows or has seen your screen lock, such as a partner or family member: anything that opens with the screen lock won't stop them. You need a hiding place with its own separate lock.
  • A repair shop, a lost phone or a USB cable: files that are only hidden can be copied off and played elsewhere. Encryption is what keeps them unreadable outside the app.

Using an app to hide videos: what to look for

Search for "hide video app" and you'll find hundreds. Many are fine; some only rename files or move them into a folder starting with a dot, which any file manager can show with one setting. Before you trust one with personal videos, check:

  • Does it encrypt the files? Not "hide" or "protect", but encrypt, on the phone.
  • Does it need an account or internet access? Every server involved is another place your videos could end up.
  • Is its lock separate from your screen lock?
  • What happens if you forget the lock? Either there's a recovery method you hold, or the developer can get in, which means others might too.
  • Can it play videos inside the app? Exporting a video to watch it puts a plain copy back on the phone.

Hiding videos with Sealcloak

Sealcloak is an offline vault for Android 8.0 or newer. It's built for exactly this job, so here's what it does and doesn't do.

  • Encrypted on the phone. Every video, photo, document, audio file and note is encrypted with AES-256-GCM. Copied off over USB, the files are unreadable. The security page explains how the keys work.
  • No account, no internet. No sign-up, email or phone number, and the app has no internet permission, so it can't upload anything.
  • Its own lock. A pattern on a 5x5 grid of at least six dots, separate from your screen lock, with optional fingerprint or face unlock.
  • Record straight into the vault. Videos taken from inside the app are saved into the vault, not the gallery. It uses your phone's camera app, and some camera apps keep their own copy, so check your gallery once.
  • A way back in. Each vault has a 9-word recovery phrase. Nobody, including the developer, can open a vault without the pattern or the phrase.
  • Screenshots and screen recording are blocked inside the app.

The honest limits: when you add videos, Sealcloak offers to keep the originals, and you delete them from the gallery yourself. It can't reach into WhatsApp or other apps, so videos saved there have to be added to the vault and then deleted in that app. There's no cloud backup; instead, make encrypted backups to a folder you choose. The free version holds 10 videos; Pro removes the limits. More on the full feature list is on the features page.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Forgetting the trash. Deleted videos sit in the gallery's and Google Photos' trash, often for 30 days or more. Empty both.
  • Leaving the chat copy. The WhatsApp or Telegram copy is the one people most often miss.
  • Moving huge files on low storage. Copying a long video before deleting the original needs room for both. Free space first so the move doesn't fail halfway.
  • Choosing Copy instead of Move. In Secure Folder and many other tools, Copy leaves the original in the gallery.
  • Not testing playback. Play the video once from its new place before deleting anything else.
  • Losing the only copy. If the video matters, keep an encrypted backup. A broken phone shouldn't take it with it.

Bottom line

To hide videos on Android, the quickest free options are Google Photos Locked Folder, Files by Google Safe folder, Samsung's Private album or Secure Folder, and Private space on Android 15. They're good against casual snooping. If the person you're worried about knows your screen lock, or the phone might leave your hands, pick a place with its own lock and real encryption, such as an offline vault. And whatever you choose, chase down the extra copies. For photos specifically, our guide on how to hide photos on Android goes through each method in detail.
