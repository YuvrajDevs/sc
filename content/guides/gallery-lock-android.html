Gallery lock on Android: every way to lock your photos

Android doesn’t have one switch that locks the whole gallery. It has several half-solutions, and which ones you get depends on your phone. Here is each one, how to turn it on, and what it actually protects.

Quick answer

To lock your gallery on Android, move private photos into a locked place: your phone’s private album (Samsung Private album or Secure Folder, Xiaomi private album, OPPO, realme and OnePlus Private Safe), Google Photos Locked Folder, Android 15’s Private space, or a gallery lock app. Only a vault app that encrypts the files works the same on every phone and keeps them unreadable even outside the app.

First: hidden, locked or encrypted?

People say “gallery lock” for three different things, and the difference decides which option is right for you.

  • Hidden means the photos don’t show in the gallery grid. Anyone who knows where the “hidden” or “un-hide” option is can see them again in seconds, with no password.
  • Locked means the photos sit behind a password, pattern or fingerprint. This stops someone scrolling through your phone after you hand it over.
  • Encrypted means the files themselves are scrambled, so they stay unreadable even if someone copies them off the phone, plugs it into a computer, or opens the storage with a file manager.

If you only want to tidy up your gallery, hiding is enough. If someone you live with picks up your phone, you need it locked. If the photos would cause real harm in the wrong hands, you want them encrypted as well.

How to lock the gallery on your phone

Most big Android brands now ship a private album of some kind. The menu names change between software versions, so if a step doesn’t match your phone exactly, search your phone’s Settings for the feature name.

Samsung Galaxy

Phones with One UI 8.5 have a Private album inside the Gallery app. Samsung says it’s available on selected Galaxy models.

  1. Open a photo or video in Gallery.
  2. Tap More (⋮), then Move to private album.
  3. Open the private album from the Gallery menu. It unlocks with your fingerprint or your screen lock, and screenshots are blocked inside it.

On older Galaxy phones, use Secure Folder: move photos into it, and they leave the main gallery and sit behind a separate lock. The Gallery’s Hide albums option is not a lock. It only removes the album tile from the list, and anyone can switch it back on.

Xiaomi, Redmi and POCO

  1. In Settings, open Face data & screen lock (or similar) and set a Privacy protection password.
  2. In Gallery, select photos, tap the menu and move them to the private album.
  3. To open it, go to Albums, pull the list down and draw your pattern.

Be careful with one thing: clearing the Gallery app’s data from Settings can remove hidden items for good. Don’t do it unless you have a backup.

OPPO, realme and OnePlus

  1. Go to Settings → Security and privacy → Private Safe and set your privacy password.
  2. In Photos, long-press a picture, tap the three-dot menu and choose Set as private.
  3. Open Private Safe again to see the photos. Fingerprint or face unlock can stand in for the password.

vivo, Motorola, Pixel and others

vivo phones have a privacy and encryption area in Settings that can protect files and apps. Search Settings for “privacy” or “encrypt” to find it on your model. Pixel and Motorola phones rely on Google’s options below.

Google Photos Locked Folder (any Android phone)

If you use Google Photos as your gallery, Locked Folder is the simplest lock that works everywhere.

  1. Open Google Photos and go to Collections (or Library), then Utilities (on some versions Collections shows it directly).
  2. Tap Locked Folder, then Set up, and confirm with your screen lock.
  3. Choose whether to back the folder up to your Google account.
  4. Select photos, tap More and choose Move to Locked Folder.

Photos in the folder don’t appear in the grid, in albums or in other apps, and screenshots are blocked inside it. The catch: it opens with your phone’s screen lock, so anyone who knows your PIN can open it too. And if you turn backup off, the photos exist only on that phone.

Android 15 Private space

On Android 15 and newer, Settings → Security & privacy → Private space creates a separate locked area for apps. You can install a gallery app or a vault inside it, give the space its own lock, and hide the space completely when it’s locked.

It’s strong, but it’s heavier than most people need for a few photos, and it only exists on newer phones and Android versions.

Why the phone’s own lock often isn’t enough

Most built-in options open with the same PIN or pattern that unlocks your phone. That’s convenient, but the people most likely to look are often the ones who already know it: a partner, a parent, a sibling, a friend you lent the phone to.

A separate lock that only you know fixes that. It also matters what happens behind the lock. Some gallery lock apps simply hide files in a different folder and put a password screen in front. Anyone with a file manager or a computer can find them. A real vault encrypts each file, so a copy taken out of the phone is useless without your key.

How to choose a gallery lock app

The Play Store is full of “gallery lock” and “photo locker” apps, and they differ far more than their icons suggest. Look for these:

  • Real encryption, stated plainly. “AES-256” on its own isn’t enough. The app should say the files themselves are encrypted on the phone, not just hidden.
  • A lock separate from your phone’s. Otherwise you’re back to the shared-PIN problem.
  • No account and no forced cloud. An app that uploads your private photos to its own servers has moved the risk, not removed it. See our guide to photo vault apps without an account.
  • A way back in. A recovery phrase or code you write down, plus encrypted backups, so a forgotten lock or a lost phone doesn’t cost you everything.
  • Few or no ads. Ads mean network access and often trackers inside an app that holds your most private files.

Forgot your gallery lock password?

This is one of the most common searches, so plan for it before it happens.

  • Phone’s own private album or Secure Folder: usually resets through your screen lock or the maker’s account (Samsung or Xiaomi account). Try the “forgot password” link on the lock screen of that album.
  • Google Photos Locked Folder: it uses your screen lock, so there’s no separate password to forget.
  • Apps that only hide files: may offer an email reset. That convenience is also a sign the files aren’t properly encrypted.
  • Apps that encrypt: can’t be reset by anyone, including the developer. Keep the recovery phrase or code safe the day you set it up.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Trusting “hide album”. It hides the tile, not the photos.
  • Forgetting the originals. After moving photos into any vault, check the gallery, Google Photos and WhatsApp folders for copies.
  • Clearing app data or factory resetting without a backup. Many locked albums live only on the phone.
  • Using the same PIN as your phone. It defeats the point if others know it.

Bottom line

For a quick lock against casual scrolling, your phone’s private album or Google Photos Locked Folder is fine.
