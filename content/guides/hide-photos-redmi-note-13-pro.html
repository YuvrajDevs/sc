How to hide photos in Redmi Note 13 Pro (and what hiding really protects)

You want a few pictures out of the main gallery on your Redmi Note 13 Pro, so they don't appear when you show someone a holiday photo or hand the phone to a child. Xiaomi gives you two built-in ways to hide photos in Redmi Note 13 Pro, and they behave very differently. This guide walks through both, shows where each one falls short, and explains when a separate encrypted vault is the better choice.

Quick answer

Open Gallery, select the photos, tap the three-dot menu, choose Add to album and pick Private album. Set a privacy password when asked. To see them again, open the Albums tab, swipe down and enter that password. The other option, long-pressing an album and tapping Hide, only removes the album from view and has no lock at all.

Step-by-step: move photos to the private album

These steps follow Xiaomi's own support pages for current Xiaomi and Redmi models running HyperOS, which is what a Redmi Note 13 Pro or Note 13 Pro+ runs after its updates. The same steps work on the Redmi Note 13 and Redmi 13. Older phones such as the Note 9 Pro or Note 8 Pro on MIUI use the same idea; a few labels differ.

  1. Open the Gallery app and pick your photos. In the Photos tab, long-press one picture, then tap any others you want to hide. You can select videos the same way.
  2. Tap the three-dot icon, then Add to album. On most versions the three dots sit in the lower right corner of the screen once something is selected.
  3. Choose Private album. If you have never used it, the phone asks you to set a privacy protection password first. On many versions you can also set it beforehand in Settings, under the screen lock section (look for Privacy protection password).
  4. Check the result. Go back to the Photos tab and scroll to where the pictures were. They should be gone from the timeline and from the album they came from.
  5. To view them later, open Albums and swipe down. A pull-down gesture on the Albums screen brings up the password or pattern prompt, then your private photos.

To bring a photo back, open the private album, select it and move it to a normal album. It returns to the main gallery.

Hiding a whole album: quick, but not a lock

The second built-in method is for whole albums, such as a folder your messaging app saves into, or a set of screenshots.

  1. Open Gallery and go to Albums.
  2. Press and hold the album, tap Hide, then tap OK.
  3. To show it again, tap the three-dot icon, open Settings, then View hidden albums, and unhide it.

Notice what's missing: no password. Anyone who picks up your unlocked phone and knows where Gallery settings are can show the album in about five seconds. It's tidy, not private. Use it to keep clutter out of sight, not to protect anything.

Three things the private album doesn't cover

Here's what most "hide photos in Redmi" videos leave out.

1. Copies that already left the phone

If Google Photos backup was on, the picture may already be in your Google account. Hiding it in the Xiaomi Gallery does nothing to that copy, and it will appear in Google Photos on any phone, tablet or browser signed in to the same account. Open Google Photos, search for the picture, and delete it there or move it into that app's Locked Folder. The same applies to photos you sent in a chat: the other person and the chat app still have them.

2. The phone's own codes

On many HyperOS versions you can let fingerprint or face unlock open the private album. That's convenient, but it also means anyone who can unlock your phone with your face while you sleep, or who you've let add a fingerprint, may get in. If a partner or family member knows your screen lock, make sure the privacy password is different, and think twice before linking it to biometrics. Our guide to what to do if someone went through your phone covers the signs to look for.

3. Clearing data and resetting

The private album belongs to the Gallery app. If you clear Gallery's storage in Settings, or the phone is reset, you can lose what's inside. If you forget the privacy password, the way back usually runs through your Xiaomi Account, much like the reset in our Xiaomi App Lock forgot password guide. Back up the photos you care about before trying anything drastic.

A separate vault with its own pattern

If you want the photos to stay private even from someone who knows your phone's codes, use a vault that doesn't rely on them. Sealcloak is an offline vault for Android 8.0 or newer, so it runs on every Redmi Note 13 model. Files you add are encrypted on the phone with AES-256-GCM, and the vault opens only with its own pattern on a 5x5 grid of at least six dots. Fingerprint or face unlock is optional, and the pattern always still works.

It needs no account, email or phone number. It has no internet permission, so nothing leaves your Redmi, and there is no cloud copy for anyone to find in a browser. If you forget the pattern, a 9-word recovery phrase gets you back in; lose both and nobody can open that vault, including the developer. The security page explains how the keys work.

A few features matter on a shared phone:

  • Intruder photos: if you turn them on, the front camera takes a picture from the third wrong pattern in a row, and you see it only after unlocking.
  • Separate vaults: up to three vaults on the free plan, each with its own pattern, and nothing shows how many exist.
  • Private camera: take photos from inside the app and they go straight into the vault rather than the gallery. It uses your camera app, so check once that MIUI or HyperOS Camera hasn't kept its own copy.
  • Screenshots blocked inside the app, so nobody can quietly screen-record your vault.

Two honest limits. Sealcloak doesn't hide other apps; it can only disguise itself, for example with the Pro calculator disguise. And it doesn't reach into your gallery on its own: when you add photos it offers to keep the originals, and you then delete them from Gallery yourself and empty the Recently deleted (or Trash) album. Mistakes to avoid on a Redmi

  • Forgetting Recently deleted. When you delete originals, Xiaomi Gallery keeps them in a Recently deleted or Trash album for a while, depending on the version. Empty it, or the photos are one tap from coming back.
  • Trusting the Hide option. A hidden album has no password. Use the private album or a vault for anything that matters.
  • Renaming folders with a dot. Old tricks such as naming a folder ".private" stop some galleries showing it, but every file manager and computer still can. It isn't protection.
  • Installing "hide photos" apps you haven't checked. Read the Play Store data safety section first. Some ask for an account or upload your pictures to their servers, which defeats the point.
  • Clearing Gallery's data to fix a glitch. If the Gallery misbehaves, restart the phone or update the app first. Clearing its storage can remove private album content.
  • Using the same code everywhere. If your screen lock, App lock and privacy password are all the same, one shoulder-surf opens everything.

Hiding apps on the Redmi Note 13 too

People who hide photos often want to hide an app as well, such as a chat app or the vault itself. Recent Redmi phones include an App lock under Settings, then Apps, and many HyperOS versions offer a Hidden apps option there. The exact wording moves around between versions, so the fastest route is the search bar at the top of Settings. Our guide to hiding apps on Android covers the general methods and their limits.

Bottom line

To hide photos in Redmi Note 13 Pro quickly, the Gallery's private album is the right built-in tool: select, Add to album, Private album, done. Skip the Hide album option for anything sensitive, because it has no lock. Then check Google Photos and Recently deleted so no copy is left behind. If the person you want privacy from knows your phone's codes, or you want the files encrypted and away from any cloud, move them into an offline vault with its own pattern.
