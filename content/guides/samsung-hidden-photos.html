Samsung hidden photos: how to hide, find and lock them on a Galaxy

Samsung hidden photos can live in four different places on a Galaxy phone, and each one behaves differently. This guide shows where to look if you are trying to find them, how to hide pictures properly if you want them gone from view, and which option actually needs a password.

Quick answer

On a Samsung phone, hidden photos are usually in one of three places: a hidden album (Gallery → Albums → ⋮ → Hide albums), the Private album in the Gallery menu on One UI 8.5, or the Gallery inside Secure Folder. Hidden albums have no lock at all. Private album opens with your screen lock. Secure Folder can use its own separate lock, which makes it the strongest of the built-in options.

The four places a Galaxy hides photos

Samsung has added privacy features to the Gallery over the years without removing the old ones, so the word “hidden” can mean quite different things on the same phone.

  • Hidden albums. A switch in the Gallery that removes an album tile from the album list. The photos are not locked, moved or protected. It is a tidying tool.
  • Private album. Introduced with One UI 8.5 on selected Galaxy models. Photos you move here leave the main grid and need your fingerprint or screen lock to open. Samsung says screenshots are blocked inside it.
  • Secure Folder. A separate, locked space on the phone with its own apps, including its own Gallery. It can have a different PIN, pattern or password from your phone, and the folder itself can be hidden from the Apps screen.
  • Google Photos Locked Folder. Not a Samsung feature, but many Galaxy owners use Google Photos as well, and photos moved there vanish from both galleries.

If you are looking for missing pictures, check all four. If you are trying to hide pictures, the rest of this page will help you pick the right one.

How to see hidden photos in Samsung Gallery

Start with the easy one. These steps follow Samsung’s own support pages; labels move slightly between One UI versions, and newer versions may say Select albums to show instead of Hide albums.

  1. Open Gallery and tap Albums. If you see a View all option, tap it.
  2. Tap the three-dot More menu, then Hide albums. You will see a list of every album with a switch next to each.
  3. Turn the switch for a hidden album back on. The album returns to the list right away, with all its photos.
  4. Check Private album. On One UI 8.5, tap the menu button at the bottom right of Gallery and look for Private album. It asks for your fingerprint or screen lock.
  5. Check Secure Folder. Open it from the Apps screen, unlock it, and tap the Gallery icon inside. If you cannot find the Secure Folder icon, swipe down with two fingers and look for its tile in Quick Settings.
  6. Check the trash. In Gallery, open the menu and look for Recycle bin or Trash. Deleted photos sit there for a while before they are removed for good.

Still missing? Open My Files and look in the DCIM and Pictures folders. Folders that contain a file called .nomedia are skipped by gallery apps, which is how some third-party “hide” apps work. My Files has a setting to show hidden files if you need to dig further.

How to hide photos on a Samsung phone

Private album (One UI 8.5)

  1. Open Gallery and select the photos or videos.
  2. Tap More (⋮), then Move to private album, and confirm with Move.
  3. The first time, Gallery walks you through setup from Menu → Private album and asks you to confirm a screen lock.

Secure Folder (most Galaxy phones)

  1. Set up Secure Folder first, from Settings → Security and privacy → More security settings → Secure Folder. Choose a lock type when asked.
  2. In Gallery, long-press a photo, select any others, tap More (⋮) and choose Move to Secure Folder.
  3. Open Secure Folder and tap its Gallery to check they arrived.
  4. Optional: in the same Secure Folder settings, turn off Add Secure Folder to Apps screen and confirm Hide. The icon disappears, and you open it again from Quick Settings.

Choose Move, not copy. A copy leaves the original in your main Gallery, which defeats the point. Samsung also advises keeping a copy of important files somewhere else before moving them, and notes that Secure Folder locks itself if the phone is rooted or running a custom system.

Hide albums

Use the same Hide albums screen from the section above and switch an album off. It is quick, but remember it only removes the tile. The photos are not locked or moved, and anyone who knows the menu can bring them back.

Why the lock matters more than the hiding

Most people who look through a phone without permission are not strangers. They are partners, parents, siblings or friends, and they often already know the phone’s PIN because they have watched it being typed a hundred times. Any “hidden” place that opens with that same PIN only stops people who do not know it.

That is why Secure Folder is worth the extra setup: give it a lock you have never used anywhere else. Private album is convenient and blocks screenshots, but it shares the screen lock. Hidden albums are fine for clearing clutter, like receipts and memes, but not for anything that would hurt if seen.

The second question is what happens to the files themselves. Samsung builds Secure Folder on its Knox security platform, and Samsung’s Private album sits inside the Gallery app. A dedicated vault takes a different approach and encrypts every file with a key that comes from a lock only you know. A separate vault on a Samsung phone

If you want a lock that is not your phone’s PIN and do not want to depend on Samsung’s account or software version, a vault app is the other route. Sealcloak works the same on every Galaxy running Android 8.0 or newer:

  • It opens with its own pattern on a 5×5 grid, with optional fingerprint unlock. Your phone’s PIN does not open it.
  • Every photo and video is encrypted on the phone with AES-256-GCM. Read how on the security page.
  • It has no account and no internet permission, so it cannot send your photos anywhere.
  • Screenshots and screen recording are blocked inside the app.
  • Optional intruder photos: from the third wrong pattern in a row, the front camera takes a picture you can see after unlocking.
  • A 9-word recovery phrase gets you back in if you forget the pattern. Nobody else, including the developer, can open the vault without it.

To move photos in, tap +, pick them from your gallery, and check they open inside the vault. Sealcloak offers to keep the originals, so delete them from Samsung Gallery yourself afterwards, then empty the Gallery’s Recycle bin. The general steps are in how to hide photos on Android.

Mistakes Galaxy owners make with hidden photos

  • Trusting Hide albums with something sensitive. It has no password. Two taps undo it.
  • Copying instead of moving. The original stays in the main Gallery and in any cloud sync you have turned on.
  • Forgetting the Recycle bin. Deleted originals wait there before they are gone. Empty it.
  • Forgetting other copies. WhatsApp, Telegram and download folders keep their own copies of shared pictures.
  • Using your phone PIN for Secure Folder. It works, but it gives the same people the same access.
  • Factory resetting without a backup. Photos kept only on the phone, in any of these places, are erased with it. Plan for backups before you change phones.

Bottom line

Samsung hidden photos are almost always in Hide albums, Private album or Secure Folder, and you can check all three in a couple of minutes. For actually keeping pictures private, use Secure Folder with its own lock, or a vault app that encrypts files and opens with a lock only you know. Hidden albums are for tidying, not privacy.
