Realme app lock: set it up, reset it, and know its limits

You want WhatsApp, your photos or your banking app shut when someone borrows your phone. Realme app lock is built into most realme phones, so there's nothing to install. This guide shows where realme puts the setting, how to set it up step by step, how to reset a forgotten app lock password, and what the lock doesn't cover.

Quick answer

On a realme phone, first set a privacy password (realme UI 5.0 and 6.0: Settings, Security and privacy, More security and privacy, Privacy password). Then open Settings, type App lock in the search bar, open App lock, enter the privacy password and switch on each app you want locked. You can unlock with the password, fingerprint or face, and choose whether apps relock when the screen locks or as soon as you leave them.

Where realme keeps app lock and the privacy password

Realme app lock depends on one thing: the privacy password. realme describes it as the code that unlocks App lock, Hide apps and Private Safe. It's separate from your screen lock, and realme says it is kept in the phone's security module, where neither the system nor apps can read it.

Where the privacy password lives depends on your realme UI version, which you can see in Settings under About device:

  • realme UI 5.0 and 6.0: Settings, then Security and privacy, then More security and privacy, then Privacy password.
  • realme UI 3.0 and 4.0: Settings, then Password & security, then Privacy password.
  • realme UI 1.0 and 2.0: Settings, then Fingerprint, face & passcode, then Privacy passcode.

App lock itself moves around between versions too, so realme's own help gives one route for all of them: open Settings and type "App lock" into the search bar. That's the fastest way, and it works even when menu names on your phone don't match a guide.

One important exception: realme says the R, S, T, Go and U editions of realme UI, used on some entry-level models, don't support App lock, Hide apps or Private Safe. If the search finds nothing, check your edition in About device.

Step-by-step: how to set up app lock in realme

  1. Set a privacy password. Use the path for your version above. realme asks you to set a security question first. On newer versions you can also add a recovery email, and on realme UI 3.0 and 4.0 a linked Google account or phone number. Set at least one; it's your way back in later.
  2. Open Settings and search for App lock. Tap App lock in the results.
  3. Enter the privacy password. realme then lists the apps on your phone, each with its own toggle.
  4. Switch on the apps that need a lock. Good candidates: WhatsApp, Telegram, Messages, Photos, Files, Gmail and any banking or payment app.
  5. Add fingerprint or face unlock. realme lets a fingerprint or your face open locked apps, so you aren't typing the code ten times a day. Typing the privacy password always remains an option.
  6. Choose when apps lock again. realme offers two options: lock when the screen locks, or lock when you exit the app. On realme UI 6.0, tap the three dots in the top right of the App lock screen, then Lock app when, and pick Exiting the app if you want the tighter setting.
  7. Check it works. Swipe the app away from Recents, turn the screen off and on, then tap the app icon. A password, fingerprint or face prompt should appear before anything loads.

The "exiting the app" option is worth choosing. With "when the screen locks", a locked app you opened a minute ago stays open for anyone you hand the phone to, until the screen goes off.

Realme app lock, Hide apps and Private Safe compared

Realme has three privacy tools on the same password, and people mix them up. Here's what each one actually does.

Hide apps is in Settings under Security and privacy (Privacy on realme UI 3.0), then Hide apps. The first time, you set an access code that starts and ends with #, for example #2580#. To see hidden apps again, type that code in the phone's dialer. realme notes some apps, such as the dialer itself, can't be hidden. For other brands, read hiding apps on Android.

Private Safe is in Settings under Security & privacy, then Private safe, on realme UI 4.0 and newer. You can also open Files, select a file, tap More and choose Move to Safe. Files moved there disappear from Photos, and realme blocks screenshots inside the safe. To bring a photo back, long-press it in the safe and choose Set as public.

Where realme app lock falls short (the part most guides skip)

Think of App lock as a guard at the entrance of each app. It's a good answer to a sibling who keeps opening your chats. What it isn't is a lockbox for your files, and the difference matters if private photos are the real worry.

  • The photos themselves aren't locked. Lock Photos, and your pictures still sit in normal storage. Files, any app with photo access, or a computer over USB can still show them. Lock Files too, and think about which other apps have photo permission.
  • Notifications slip past the lock. A WhatsApp or SMS preview can appear on the lock screen or when someone pulls down the shade, even though the app is locked. Set those apps to hide notification content.
  • The relock window. With "lock when the screen locks", an app stays open until the screen goes off.
  • One code, three doors. App lock, Hide apps and Private Safe all open with the same privacy password. Whoever learns it can get into all three.
  • Cloud copies aren't covered. If Google Photos backs up your camera, anyone signed in to that Google account on another device can browse those pictures, lock or no lock.

So use realme app lock for what it's good at, your apps, and handle files separately. The surest way to keep a picture private is to move it somewhere the gallery can't reach. When the photos need more than an app lock

Maybe your realme is one of the editions with no App lock, or maybe you want the files themselves protected rather than the Photos app. That's the job Sealcloak was made for: an offline, encrypted vault on Android 8.0 and up for photos, videos, PDFs and other documents, audio, text notes and scans. It installs on a realme like on any Android phone.

  • Nothing to sign up for. You never give an email, a phone number or a name. Sealcloak doesn't even request the internet permission, so your files have no route off the phone.
  • Encrypted, not just hidden. Files are scrambled with AES-256-GCM before they're stored. Plug the phone into a laptop and the vault folder is unreadable. Details are on the security page.
  • A separate key from realme's. You open the vault by drawing a pattern of six or more dots on a 5x5 grid, so knowing your privacy password doesn't help anyone. Fingerprint or face unlock can be added.
  • Know if someone tried. Turn on intruder photos and the front camera snaps whoever gets the pattern wrong three times running. Only you see the picture, after unlocking.
  • Recovery that stays yours. Every vault comes with a 9-word recovery phrase. It's the only spare key: the developer has no copy, so keep the phrase somewhere safe.

What it won't do: Sealcloak has no power over other apps, so realme app lock is still the tool for WhatsApp and Messages. It also won't pull pictures out of Photos on its own. You pick the files to bring in, it asks whether to keep the originals, and clearing them from the gallery is up to you. Pair the two: App lock for apps, the vault for files. Forgot your realme app lock password?

Because App lock uses the privacy password, resetting one resets the other. realme lists three ways back in:

  1. Security question. On the privacy password screen, tap the forgot option and answer the question you chose during setup.
  2. Secure (recovery) email. If you added one, you can verify through that address instead.
  3. Linked realme account. If the phone is signed in to a realme account linked to the privacy password, you can verify with it.

Then set a new privacy password and check your locked apps, hidden apps and Private Safe still look right. If none of the options work, take the phone to a realme service centre. Avoid two popular shortcuts. Wiping a locked app's storage in Settings deletes its chats and data for good, and sideloaded "app lock remover" files can carry malware. A forgotten code is the smaller problem. The Xiaomi version of this problem works much the same way on Redmi and POCO phones.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Reusing your lock screen PIN. Someone who has seen you unlock the phone over your shoulder would then open every locked app too.
  • Locking Photos but not Files. The same pictures are usually one tap away in the file manager.
  • Skipping the recovery options. Without a security question, email or linked account, a forgotten code means a trip to a service centre.
  • Forgetting notification content. Previews of new messages show up whether or not the chat app is locked.
  • Treating Hide apps as a lock. Anyone who knows or guesses the # code sees the apps; lock them too.

Bottom line

Realme app lock runs on the privacy password. Set it with a security question and a recovery option, then search Settings for App lock, enter the code and toggle on every app worth guarding, with Photos and Files near the top of the list. Pick "lock when exiting the app" for the tightest setting. It costs nothing and does a fine job of keeping casual hands out of your apps. Your pictures, though, stay readable to a laptop, a file manager or a cloud backup, so put anything truly private in Private Safe or an encrypted offline vault as well.
